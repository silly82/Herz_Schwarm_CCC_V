# Prior art: how event badges and wearable LED swarms networked hundreds to thousands of devices (as of October 2026)

Scope note: notes collected 2026-10-17 for Herz-Schwarm (ESP32-S3 LED hearts, ESP-NOW broadcast at 20 packets/s per badge, RSSI proximity, base-station-seeded "virus"/"wave" effects; 12 badges today, a few hundred at CCC Camp 2027). Every claim below has an inline source. Single-source, forum, or marketing claims are marked as such. Inferences are kept apart from findings.

## 1. CCC event badges (r0ket 2011, rad1o 2015, card10 2019, flow3r 2023, Congress badges, Camp 2027): how did they network, and what was learned?

### Takeaway
The most relevant CCC precedent is the **r0ket (Camp 2011)**. Its 2.4 GHz "mesh" was not a routed mesh. Each badge kept a small store of state (10 slots of 32 bytes) and re-broadcast the whole store on a jittered timer. The newest timestamp won for each message type, and a "generation" counter reset everything network-wide. A network-wide "nice" knob made badges randomly skip sends to cut traffic. Later CCC camp badges did little badge-to-badge networking. card10 (2019) shipped BLE as a peripheral only, connecting to a phone. flow3r (2023) had no badge-to-badge radio feature, and its Wi-Fi UI arrived only after camp. The 39C3 (Dec 2025) "friends" LED badge used IR, not radio. I found no announced Camp 2027 badge.

### Cited Findings
**r0ket, Chaos Communication Camp 2011**
- The r0ket was the camp badge. It was billed as a full microcontroller dev board as well as a name tag, with an ARM Cortex-M3, a LiPo battery and a "2.4 GHz radio interface" — [Camp 2011 Fahrplan](https://fahrplan.events.ccc.de/camp/2011/Fahrplan/events/4564.en.html); [r0ket concept page](https://r0ket.badge.events.ccc.de/concept)
- Mesh constants in the firmware: `MESH_CHANNEL 83`, `MESH_MAC "ORBIT"`, `MESHBUFSIZE 10` (10 stored messages), `MESHPKTSIZE 32` (32-byte packets), `M_SENDINT 500`, `M_RECVINT 1000`, `M_RECVTIM 100` (units not stated, probably ms). The packet layout is byte 0 = type, byte 1 = generation, bytes 2–5 = 32-bit timestamp, then the body — [r0ket firmware mesh.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/r0ket/r0ket/master/firmware/funk/mesh.h)
- How it works: on each send cycle a badge refreshes its own time packet (slot 0) and **retransmits every stored packet**. A received packet replaces the stored packet of the same type only if its timestamp is strictly newer. Packets from another generation are dropped. A newer generation (8-bit wraparound compare) resets time sync, which is how the whole swarm gets reset. When the buffer is full, a random slot is evicted. Send and receive intervals are randomised to roughly 0.5–1.5× the nominal value. A `meshnice` value carried in the time packet makes nodes skip sends at random to reduce traffic. A badge with no valid time adopts the first time packet it accepts and then stops adjusting. "B" packets carry a hop counter and the forwarder's UUID — [r0ket firmware mesh.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/r0ket/r0ket/master/firmware/funk/mesh.c)
- The same source has a bug: `!meshbuffer[i].flags&MF_USED` is an operator-precedence error (my reading of the code via the fetch tool, not a documented project issue) — [mesh.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/r0ket/r0ket/master/firmware/funk/mesh.c)

**OpenBeacon "Sputnik", 24C3 (2007) / Camp 2007 / 25C3 (2008): earlier CCC precedent for hundreds of 2.4 GHz wearables**
- Sputnik tags used a Nordic nRF24L01 2.4 GHz chip with a PCB antenna and one CR2032 coin cell. More than 30 OpenBeacon base stations in the venue received them — [CCC Sputnik page](https://events.ccc.de/congress/2007/Sputnik/index.html)
- About 1,000 tags at €10 were brought for roughly 3,000 attendees. All but the 100 kept for demos were sold. The tracking data was later published — [RFID Journal](https://www.rfidjournal.com/people-tracking-experiment-offers-insights-into-rfid-privacy-concerns)
- At 25C3 (2008), OpenAMD and SocioPatterns added "who is near whom" social features on top of the tracking — [events.ccc.de 2008](https://events.ccc.de/en/2008/12/05/projects-you-have-nowhere-to-hide-from-openbeacon-with-openamd-or-do-you/)

**rad1o, Camp 2015**
- An SDR badge. Search snippets say about 4,500 were handed out. I did not verify this figure against a primary source — [Wikipedia: Electronic badge (search snippet)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_badge)

**card10, Camp 2019**
- The CPU is a **MAX32666**, a dual-core Cortex-M4F at 96 MHz with Bluetooth 5. It is not an nRF part, contrary to the assumption in the brief. The battery is 200 mAh, and there are 11 upward and 4 downward RGB LEDs — [card10 hardware page](https://card10.badge.events.ccc.de/hardware/); [Analog Devices MAX32666](https://www.analog.com/en/products/MAX32666.html)
- The card10 implements **only the BLE peripheral role**, with one connection at a time. BLE is off by default, and enabling it restarts the device. There are no BLE privacy features, so a paired card10 can be tracked. The Android companion app sets time, installs apps and sets the "personal state". iOS only gets time setting — [card10 BLE user guide](https://card10.badge.events.ccc.de/userguide/ble/)
- The "personal state" LED was meant to signal how open you are to interaction. You set it locally or over BLE (from firmware v1.6), and it keeps glowing after you leave the app — [card10 talk Camp 2019](https://media.ccc.de/v/Camp2019-10365-card10_badge); [card10 logbook](https://card10.badge.events.ccc.de/logbook/)
- Receiving BLE advertisements, used by the Exposure Notification "curiosity" app, came only later through a merge request labelled untested. The app counts unique MACs seen in the last 100 s — [card10 Exposure Notifications](https://card10.badge.events.ccc.de/interhacktions/exposure_notifications/)

**flow3r, Camp 2023**
- ESP32-S3 with 8 MiB RAM and 16 MiB flash, programmed in MicroPython. It is positioned as an instrument that turns touch into sound and light — [events.ccc.de flow3r announcement](https://events.ccc.de/en/2023/06/05/camp23-the-flow3r-badge/)
- The Wi-Fi connection UI (`w1f1` app) came in firmware **1.3.0, dated 2023-11-26**, months after camp. An earlier changelog entry added auto-connect to "Camp WiFi" and a "Get Apps" downloader. I found no badge-to-badge radio feature — [flow3r CHANGELOG 1.3.0](https://git.flow3r.garden/flow3r/flow3r-firmware/-/raw/release/1.3.0/CHANGELOG.md)

**39C3 (Hamburg, 27–30 Dec 2025)**
- The "39C3 LED badge" was a beginner soldering kit: 42 LEDs, a small MCU and an **IR transmitter and receiver**. Alone it blinks randomly. Among friends (other badges nearby) it runs a chaser pattern. It also reacts to TBD-assembly badges. Materials cost about €2 per badge — [39C3 hub badge page](https://events.ccc.de/congress/2025/hub/badge/detail/37aa45fe-8624-4c62-8dba-490507ec20c2); [39C3 workshop listing](https://events.ccc.de/congress/2025/hub/en/event/detail/39c3-led-badge-smd-for-beginners-day-2)
- 39C3 also had a session on running **MeshCore (LoRa) on the WHY2025 badge**, which needed bodge-wire mods and €6 antenna kits — [39C3 hub: MeshCore on your WHY2025 badge](https://events.ccc.de/congress/2025/hub/en/event/detail/meshcore-on-your-why2025-badge)

**Camp 2027**
- The camp runs every four years (2019, 2023, so 2027 is next). Searches found no announced Camp 2027 badge — [FreeBSD Foundation CCCamp 2023 trip report](https://freebsdfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/jones_cccamp.pdf); [muCCC "Badge Frames" blog, Feb 2026](https://muc.ccc.de/en/blog/2026/02/12/badge-frames/)

**Camp and Congress radio environment**
- 36C3 (2019) NOC: about 11,000 Wi-Fi clients, 86% on 5 GHz overall and 96% at peak. The NOC said "goodbye to 2.4 GHz" — [36C3 Infrastructure Review subtitles](https://amara.org/subtitles/6qFbavdMs60r/en/2/download/36C3%20-%20%2036C3%20Infrastructure%20Review.en.srt)

### Inferences
- The r0ket design suits Herz-Schwarm's effect propagation well. It gossips **state** rather than events: each badge re-broadcasts a bounded set of "latest value per type" records, so airtime per badge stays fixed no matter how many effects are running. "Newest timestamp wins" deduplicates for free. A generation/epoch byte gives a clean global reset that a base station can trigger. A network-wide "nice" (probabilistic skip) value carried in the beacon lets organisers throttle the whole swarm at runtime. All of this fits easily into a single ESP-NOW frame.
- CCC camp badges have not shown radio swarm behaviour at scale. The two wearable-scale CCC radio deployments with numbers (Sputnik with about 1,000 tags; r0ket) both used Nordic 2.4 GHz chips with tiny packets.
- Since the camp and Congress NOC now push clients to 5 GHz, 2.4 GHz ESP-NOW competes less with the official network than it would have a decade ago. Personal hotspots, other badges and other ESP gadgets will still crowd 2.4 GHz.

### Gaps
- I found no public card10 or flow3r post-mortem talk with networking numbers (searches for 36C3 and 37C3 retrospectives found nothing).
- The r0ket mesh's real-world performance at Camp 2011 (how many badges, loss rates) was not found. Only the source code was reviewed.
- No Camp 2027 badge information has been published as far as these searches show.

## 2. DEF CON, Hackaday Supercon, EMF, SHA/MCH, WHY2025, Disobey, Fri3d, BSides and others: radio, protocol, scale, problems

### Takeaway
At the very largest scale (DEF CON official, 25k–30k badges), designers mostly **avoided RF broadcast for badge-to-badge**. They used a physical connector (DC26), near-field magnetic induction that only works at touching distance (DC27), and IR (DC32). The biggest real RF swarm at a hacker event is **Meshtastic at DEF CON 33 (2025)**, with 2,000+ nodes reported. It only worked with special event firmware: the SHORT_TURBO preset, a 3-hop cap and limited rebroadcasting. That firmware followed a 2024 Hamvention collapse caused by one MQTT bridge. **Fri3d Camp 2026** (about 700 ESP32 badges, BLE beacons) is the closest recent analogue to Herz-Schwarm. Its lessons were practical: BLE scan defaults and the camp Wi-Fi tripped them up. Hackaday Supercon 2025 (about 500 ESP32-S3 + SX1262 LoRa badges, repeat-everything mesh) is the most direct test of a dense relay mesh, but I found no post-mortem.

### Cited Findings
**DEF CON official badges**
- DC26 (2018): PIC32MM MCU, 4×AA batteries. Badge-to-badge interaction ran through a **physical connector** on the bottom edge (reader comments name Amphenol RotaConnect). Over 27,000 were rumoured made, and a reader comment says 28,200 — [Hackaday first look DC26](https://hackaday.com/2018/08/09/first-look-at-def-con-26-official-badge/)
- DC27 (2019, Joe Grand): NXP KL27 Cortex-M0+ with an **NFMI radio (NxH2261)**, 6 LEDs and a coin cell. Badges light up when held next to or very close to other badges. The game required meeting people. Manufacturing went straight from first working prototype to full production — [NXP blog](https://www.nxp.com/company/about-nxp/smarter-world-blog/BL-MEET-JOE-GRAND-AKA-KINGPIN-AT-DEF-CON-27); [Grand Idea Studio DC27 slides](https://grandideastudio.com/media/dc27_bdg_slides.pdf); [Rob Pegoraro](https://robpegoraro.com/tag/conference-badge/)
- DC32 (2024): Raspberry Pi RP2350 with touch screen, RTC, RGB LEDs, SAO and **IR communication**. A budget and contract dispute with Entropic Engineering followed, with DEF CON saying the project ran more than 60% over budget — [DEF CON shop DC32 badge](https://shop.defcon.org/products/dc32-badge); [Hackster on DC32 dispute](https://hackster.io/news/def-con-32-s-raspberry-pi-rp2350-powered-badge-sits-at-the-center-of-a-major-disagreement-05e96385a3dc)

- DC34 (Aug 2026): electronic badge built around bunnie Huang's open, IR-inspectable Baochip-1x. I found no badge-to-badge radio info — [CircuitDigest DC34](https://circuitdigest.com/news/defcon-34s-badge-had-a-hackable-open-source-chip-you-could-shine-infrared-through-to-inspect-its-design)

**Meshtastic at DEF CON (largest RF swarm at a hacker event)**
- DEF CON 33 (Aug 2025): attendees reported **more than 2,000 nodes**, called the largest known Meshtastic network. Official numbers were not published. It was the second DEF CON event firmware and the first built with organisers (Darknet-NG, Lonely Hackers Club). Problems: NodeInfo spoofing by anyone holding the channel PSK, PSK channels lacking message authentication, and a NodeDB limited to about 100 nodes in memory, so spoofed entries could cycle in. Many app and firmware bugs were found — [Meshtastic blog, 12 Aug 2025](https://meshtastic.org/blog/that-one-time-at-defcon/)
- Event firmware settings: SHORT_TURBO modem preset, "capped at 3 hops", "optimized event-mode settings for dense meshes" — [Meshtastic DC33 event firmware announcement (nostr mirror)](https://damus.io/nevent1qqspan9u2je53kztsvwen2n7m76t8f9gxpnnl9yxu8n35lwuhter5wq9dpw4n). Wikipedia adds that it limits rebroadcasting and supported "between 2,000 and 2,500 nodes" — [Wikipedia: Meshtastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meshtastic)
- Origin: at **Hamvention 2024** the mesh collapsed after one user's MQTT bridge flooded it with traffic, which led to the large-event firmware — [Wikipedia: Meshtastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meshtastic)
- An RF Village speaker at DC33 said the event build trades range for shorter, faster messages because range isn't needed in a dense venue, and that the event firmware is not usable at home — [DEF CON 33 RF Village captions](https://media.defcon.org/DEF%20CON%2033/DEF%20CON%2033%20villages%20and%20creators/DEF%20CON%2033%20villages%20and%20creators%20captions/DEF%20CON%2033%20-%20RF%20Village%20-%20Meshtastic%20Command%20%26%20Control.eng.txt)
- Follow-ups: PR #11275, merged 29 Jul 2026, makes the event-mode hop limit a build option (`USERPREFS_EVENT_MODE_HOP_LIMIT`, 0–7, default 3) — [meshtastic/firmware PR 11275](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/11275). Firmware 2.8.0 added **automatic variable hop limits based on live mesh activity and message size** — [Meshtastic 2.8.0 release notes](https://newreleases.io/project/github/meshtastic/firmware/release/v2.8.0.47db0e3). **Fri3d Camp 2026 (Belgium): closest analogue, about 700 ESP32 badges**
- About 700 people carried a badge (2024 and 2026 Fri3d badges, ESP32-based, MicroPythonOS). "Fri3d friends" uses **BLE beacons with manufacturer data** (marker, version, group IDs as a short hash of the group name, and a name). Badges count each other as friends if they share a group ID, with no server. A short GATT connection is used for contact swaps — [steeman.be, 28 Jul 2026](https://steeman.be/posts/building-fri3d-friends/)
- Measured: with default scan settings a badge heard **2 beacons in 12 s**. With an explicit interval and window at a 50% duty cycle it heard **44 in 12 s**, with peers never more than 1 s stale. The default duplicate filter made presence "flap". Other issues: pausing the app turned the radio off and wiped the GATT table (swaps worked once and then silently failed), a race produced corrupt contact data that automated tests missed, and the **Wi-Fi setup portal was unreachable on the camp network**, forcing a redesign to Web Bluetooth. Lesson: "Don't auto-assign groups, or every fresh badge would match every other one" — [steeman.be](https://steeman.be/posts/building-fri3d-friends/)

### Inferences
- Pattern at mega-scale: when tens of thousands of badges share a space, designers pick **short-range, physically gated** links (contact, NFMI, IR). They do not need MAC-layer congestion control, and "proximity" is guaranteed by physics rather than estimated from RSSI. Herz-Schwarm's proximity reaction could likewise use a deliberately short-range channel (IR, or very low TX power) separate from the timing broadcast.
- The Meshtastic DEF CON experience shows that a flooding mesh with hundreds to thousands of nodes **survives only with a hard hop cap (3), a fast PHY preset (short airtime), limited rebroadcasting, and no unbounded bridges/repeaters**. One misbehaving node (an MQTT bridge) took down Hamvention 2024. The Herz-Schwarm base station and any relays need rate limits.
- Fri3d shows that **BLE scan defaults miss most beacons**: 2 versus 44 per 12 s is about a 20× difference. If BLE adverts are ever used for presence, scan interval and window must be set explicitly and duplicate filtering disabled. Also, setup and OTA must not depend on the camp Wi-Fi network.

### Gaps
- No Supercon 2025 post-event write-up was found on whether the 500-badge LoRa repeat mesh stayed usable. This is the most relevant missing data point.
- No official Meshtastic DEF CON 32/33 channel-utilisation figures or full event-firmware parameter list (beyond SHORT_TURBO, 3 hops and limited rebroadcast) was found.
- BornHack and BSides badges with mesh features: nothing specific found.
- SHA2017 badge Wi-Fi load on the camp network: nothing found.

## 3. Synchronised LED crowds: PixMob/Xylobands, festival projects, WLED ESP-NOW sync, ESP-NOW group-sync projects, firefly sync

### Takeaway
Commercial crowd LEDs (PixMob, Xylobands) that reach tens of thousands of devices use **one-way broadcast from a central transmitter** (IR or sub-GHz RF) to dumb receivers that never transmit, so they have no collision problem at all. Hobby ESP-NOW swarms use either a master or timebase broadcast (WLED, Espressif `espnow_time`, NocturNation) or a leaderless "adopt the highest clock" rule (CrowdClock). WLED's ESP-NOW sync is reported flaky in community threads. WLED sends a **timebase** so that effect phase, not frames, is synchronised. Pulse-coupled ("firefly") sync is proven on motes (about 100 µs on lossy multi-hop MicaZ) and has been shown on ESP32-S3 + ESP-NOW (CHIRP, 2026), converging in "the first few minutes".

### Cited Findings
**PixMob**
- PixMob uses either an **IR transmitter** or an RF system, and BLE variants exist. Shows can be pre-programmed to songs and/or triggered live. Battery life is 4–7 h depending on model — [PixMob FAQ](https://pixmob.com/faq)
- IR shapes (e.g. hearts) are made by shining the IR beam through a mask, so only bands inside the beam change colour. The RF version receives "precise timing and colors for each band" — [annotated PixMob video](https://media.dltj.org/annotated-video/20240128T201746-GCsmZA08oD8-How_Concert_LED_Wristbands_Work/index.html)
- Teardown: two RGB LEDs, an unmarked MCU and EEPROM, an IR receiver and 2× CR1632 cells in series. The IR transmitter is probably on a lighting tower with line of sight to the crowd — [Hackaday PixMob teardown](https://hackaday.com/tag/abov/)
- Reverse engineering found about 100 valid IR packets on a 38 kHz subcarrier. The newer "Wave2" RF bands use 868.415 MHz — [Hackaday LED wristband](https://hackaday.com/tag/led-wristband/); [zenn.dev](https://zenn.dev/dateshim/articles/a690637f31b358); [GitHub pixmob topic](https://github.com/topics/pixmob?l=python)

**Xylobands**
- An RF receiver in each band. Controllers are a handheld unit (about 250 m) or a laptop with RF transmitter (up to 300 m). The operator can address all bands or colour subsets. First large use was Coldplay's Mylo Xyloto tour (2011/2012; the article is inconsistent). There were early rehearsal difficulties, and battery life is low — [Wikipedia: Xyloband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xyloband)
- A maker claim, from search snippets and unverified: one ground transmitter controls 50,000 bands, with 8–12 h battery and shows run from DMX timecode — [Impressive Magazine (marketing-derived)](https://impressivemagazine.com/why-led-wristbands-are-the-new-norm-at-concerts/amp/)

**WLED**
- The UDP notifier sends a 24+ byte state packet (brightness, colours, effect, speed, intensity, palette…) **only on change, never on a timer**. The only redundancy is "Send notifications twice". There are 8 sync groups. Receivers do not adopt state after boot until a new packet arrives — [WLED UDP notifier docs](https://kno.wled.ge/interfaces/udp-notifier/)
- The sync packet carries a **timebase**: the sender writes `millis() + strip.timebase` into bytes 25–28. The receiver sets `strip.timebase = t + PRESUMED_NETWORK_DELAY − millis()`, so effects run phase-aligned with no per-frame traffic. The ESP-NOW path broadcasts to `ESPNOW_BROADCAST_ADDRESS` in fragments (magic 'W', 247-byte chunks), accepts only "linked remotes", aborts on out-of-order fragments, processes at most one complete message per 250 ms, and **ignores ESP-NOW sync while connected to Wi-Fi** — [WLED udp.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wled/WLED/main/wled00/udp.cpp)
- ESP-NOW sync arrived in 0.15.0-b1 ("Sync your WLED devices without WiFi") — [WLED 0.15.0-b1 release](https://newreleases.io/project/github/wled/WLED/release/v0.15.0-b1). Community reports: not working for several users in 0.15 betas/RC. It is broadcast, not mesh, "so only devices within reach of the master will sync". In one 8-suit battery deployment ESP-NOW sync failed, so the suits ran identical playlists with a WiZ remote and a periodic "hard reset" preset to re-align drift, with range above 150 m reported — [WLED forum: ESP-NOW sync in AP mode 8 devices](https://wled.discourse.group/t/esp-now-sync-in-ap-mode-8-devices/13861); [WLED forum: sync 2 ESP32s](https://wled.discourse.group/t/sync-2-esp32s-without-wifi-router/9710)
- NocturNation (open source, forum description): one-way ESP-NOW heartbeat to unlimited receivers, which **scan multiple channels to lock onto the heartbeat**. About 100 m range, 300 m+ with relays. Forum claim only — [WLED forum](https://wled.discourse.group/t/esp-now-sync-in-ap-mode-8-devices/13861)

**ESP-NOW badge and wearable sync projects**
- CrowdClock (Tony Goacher): ESP32 with a 16-LED ring. Each badge broadcasts its local clock tick over ESP-NOW and **adopts any higher clock it hears**. There is no master, so badges joining, leaving or dying don't break sync. Display is quantised to 50 ms steps. Open source — [Hackster project](https://www.hackster.io/tony-goacher/self-synchronising-led-badges-using-esp-now-dfa2b0); [Hackaday "Building Festival Badges That Sync Themselves Up"](https://hackaday.com/blog/page/191/?cat=GUAM)
- Elektor "Fireflies": ESP-NOW beacons let nearby wearables form groups of matching colour, and newcomers adopt the group colour ("find your clan" at Burning Man-type events). The ESP8266 port needs care because blocking LED writes on a single core disturb timing — [Elektor Labs Fireflies](https://www.elektormagazine.com/labs/fireflies)
- CHIRP (UCF thesis, 2026): ESP32-S3 + ESP-NOW with peer discovery, round-robin pairing and **firefly-inspired phase synchronisation**. It converged to a stable synchronised state "within the first few minutes". Network growth had minimal impact on synchronised members, and rejoin events caused temporary disruptions that recovered — [UCF STARS](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/gradstudies_etd_2026/200)
- Espressif's `espnow_time` module: an initiator **broadcasts authoritative time and never adjusts its own clock**. Responders compute an offset and can request sync after deep sleep. The example uses a `sync_interval_ms` of 30,000 and `max_drift_ms` 100 — [Espressif ESP-NOW time sync docs](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-now/en/latest/esp32/api-reference/time/espnow_time.html)
- Pixelblaze controllers support time-sync and beacon packets so several controllers run patterns together (off by default in the Python client) — [pixelblaze-client](https://pypi.org/project/pixelblaze-client/0.9.5/)

**Burning Man and festival radio projects**
- "Unfolding Humanity" (2018): 26 Teensy 3.2 controllers on nRF24L01 radios with custom protocols, plus interacting garments — [PJRC forum](https://forum.pjrc.com/threads/53671-Teensy-3-2-at-Burning-Man-2018-Unfolding-Humanity)
- 2019 Burning Man pendant: about 60 units on SX1280 LoRa that could broadcast 8-character messages to everyone — [Hackaday.io](https://hackaday.io/project/165635-2019-burning-man-pendant/discussion-127858)
- "Burning Mesh" is a Meshtastic-based playa mesh (2025) — [Hackster](https://www.hackster.io/JingerZeng/getting-started-with-burning-mesh-2025-hello-burn-5264ee)

**Firefly / pulse-coupled oscillator (PCO) sync**
- Reachback Firefly Algorithm (Werner-Allen et al., SenSys 2005): the first firefly sync on MicaZ motes, **within 100 µs on a multi-hop indoor testbed with asymmetric, lossy links**. It handles MAC and message delays by using delayed ("reachback") information from the previous period to adjust the next firing — [Princeton SSR](https://ssr.princeton.edu/publications/firefly-inspired-sensor-network-synchronicity-realistic-radio-effects)
- Later PCO work on real radios: modified reachback with energy saving (EURASIP 2009). Reachback firefly on 802.15.4 nodes with an off-the-shelf MAC, where the shared time enables a low-duty-cycle protocol so radios sleep in silent phases (Leidenfrost 2008) — [EURASIP 2009](https://jes-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/articles/10.1155/2009/186406); [Leidenfrost 2008](https://mobile.aau.at/%7Ewelmenre/papers/leidenfrost-2008-Establishing-Wireless-Time-Triggered-Communication-using-a-Firefly-Clock-Synchronization-Approach.pdf)
- Ideal PCO assumptions (identical oscillators, instantaneous firing, every firing observed) fail on half-duplex radios. Real deployments relax them — [Tyrrell 2006 (MiNEMA)](https://mobile.aau.at/publications/tyrrell-2006-minema-firefly.pdf); [Northumbria PCO simulation](https://nrl.northumbria.ac.uk/id/eprint/36196)

### Inferences
- Herz-Schwarm sits between the two families. The heartbeat itself could be **PixMob-style**: a base station (or a few) broadcasts a phase/timebase beacon, and badges mostly listen. Badge-originated traffic would be kept to occasional state gossip. If you want "no master required", CrowdClock-style highest-clock adoption is the simplest leaderless rule. Its weakness is that one badge with a corrupted or fast clock drags everyone forward, so cap the step size or require agreement from more than one source.
- WLED's timebase approach (send "effect time", let each node render locally) is the key trick for cutting 20 Hz state broadcasts to a few Hz or less. The 250 ms minimum between processed messages also shows how to suppress floods.
- Firefly/PCO adds robustness (no leader, tolerates loss) but converges slowly: minutes on CHIRP. It is a good fallback when the base station is out of range, not a replacement for a beacon.

### Gaps
- No public PixMob or Xyloband RF protocol timing (packet rate, how phase is maintained between commands) was found beyond the 868.415 MHz Wave2 band and the IR 38 kHz subcarrier.
- The CHIRP thesis PDF (node count, message rate, accuracy) was not read. Only the abstract was.
- I found no measured sync error for WLED ESP-NOW sync or CrowdClock.

## 4. Techniques: central beacon vs decentralised consensus, reducing broadcast rate, gossip with TTL/dedup, time-slotting, RSSI proximity in crowds and better alternatives

### Takeaway
At a few hundred badges, a **20 Hz-per-badge ESP-NOW broadcast will overload one 2.4 GHz channel**. ESP-NOW's default PHY is 1 Mbps, so each frame takes on the order of a millisecond of airtime. The proven fixes: send a phase/timebase at low rate and extrapolate locally (WLED, Espressif `espnow_time`). Gossip bounded state with dedup by message ID or newest-timestamp (r0ket; Espressif's esp-now `magic` field). Use a small hop cap (Meshtastic event mode: 3) and an RSSI floor for forwarding (Espressif `forward_rssi`). Suppress redundant rebroadcasts with Trickle-style "heard k copies, stay quiet". Optionally self-organise TDMA slots (DESYNC). RSSI is a poor distance estimator in crowds: body loss of 10–30 dB, ±10 dB just from swapping who holds the device. Better ranging (Wi-Fi FTM on ESP32-S3, BLE Channel Sounding on nRF54L15) gets to roughly 0.3–1 m in good conditions, but needs pairwise exchanges, which don't scale to hundreds of peers. As of Oct 2026 no Espressif chip is confirmed to support Channel Sounding.

### Cited Findings
**ESP-NOW facts that bound the design**
- Default PHY rate 1 Mbps. Payload 250 bytes (v1) or 1470 bytes (v2). 20 peers maximum, up to 17 encrypted (default 7). **Encryption not supported for multicast/broadcast**. Rate is settable per peer (`esp_now_set_peer_rate_config`). Send and receive callbacks run in the high-priority Wi-Fi task. Power save is possible via `esp_now_set_wake_window()` plus `esp_wifi_connectionless_module_set_wake_interval()` (the default keeps RX on continuously). Channel must match the local channel — [ESP-IDF ESP-NOW docs (ESP32-S3)](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32s3/api-reference/network/esp_now.html)
- Espressif's `esp-now` component header implements gossip primitives. `magic` is a unique packet ID ("Packets with the same identifier will be filtered"). `forward_ttl` is the hop count (5-bit field, max `0x1f`). `forward_rssi`: packets received weaker than this "will not be transferred… in order to avoid network congestion". `filter_adjacent_channel` exists because HT20 frames are received from adjacent channels. `filter_weak_signal`. `retransmit_count` defaults to 10, with the comment "Too many packet retransmissions will lead to network congestion" — [espressif/esp-now espnow.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/espressif/esp-now/master/src/espnow/include/espnow.h)
- Espressif's own guide claims "hundreds of devices" via unicast, broadcast and group control, with no test data. Frequency hopping and low-power were TODO items — [esp-now User Guide](https://github.com/espressif/esp-now/blob/master/User_Guide.md)
- Espressif reliability test: 8 ESP32 nodes at 4 packets/s each to one central node in a building. About 50% of nodes were "reliable", and loss "highly depends on structure of the building". Recommendation: multiple central units and software delivery control — [Espressif developer blog](https://developer.espressif.com/blog/reliability-esp-now/)
- Latency and jitter: one ESP8266 test saw about 80 µs receive-time spread across 5 receivers but up to 5 ms sender-side jitter — [Arduino forum](https://forum.arduino.cc/t/esp-now-broadcast-transmisison-latency/1347159). Typical delivery was 5–7 ms with spikes to 50–100 ms when sends were closer than about 20 ms apart — [espressif/esp-now issue #115](https://github.com/espressif/esp-now/issues/115)
- ESPHome `espnow`: broadcast is cheaper in airtime than unicast, but every ESP-NOW device on the channel must process it, which costs battery. Broadcasts are unacknowledged, and the component does **not** implement ESP-NOW encryption. `on_broadcast` fires only for **registered peers**, and unknown senders go to a single `on_unknown_peer` trigger. The channel is forced to the Wi-Fi channel when Wi-Fi is on — [ESPHome ESP-NOW component](https://esphome.io/components/espnow)

**Rate reduction and time sync**
- The timebase-in-packet approach: WLED sends `millis()+timebase` and the receiver adds a presumed network delay — [WLED udp.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wled/WLED/main/wled00/udp.cpp). Espressif's `espnow_time` example re-broadcasts authoritative time every 30 s — [espnow_time docs](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-now/en/latest/esp32/api-reference/time/espnow_time.html)
- BLE-based ESP32 time sync to about ±1 ms has been documented. The same project cites BLE literature from 320 ns (BlueSync) to 69±71 µs (application layer), and it chose BLE over ESP-NOW because of coexistence issues — [mlehaptics docs](https://mlehaptics.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Achieving_sub_1ms_time_synchronization_over_BLE_on_ESP32/)

**Gossip, flooding control and slotting**
- Trickle (RFC 6206): parameters Imin, Imax and redundancy constant k. Suppression and randomised transmit points make the **communication rate scale logarithmically with density** — [RFC 6206](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6206). Typical k is 1–5. RPL defaults to 10, deployments found 3–5 adequate, and MPL recommends k=1 — [Adaptive Broadcast Suppression for Trickle (arXiv 1509.08664)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.08664)
- Meshtastic event mode caps hops at 3, and firmware 2.8.0 adapts the hop limit to live mesh activity and message size — [PR 11275](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware/pull/11275); [2.8.0 notes](https://newreleases.io/project/github/meshtastic/firmware/release/v2.8.0.47db0e3)
- r0ket: randomised 0.5–1.5× send intervals, a bounded 10-slot store re-broadcast each cycle, newest-timestamp-wins per type, generation reset, and a network-wide probabilistic skip ("meshnice") — [r0ket mesh.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/r0ket/r0ket/master/firmware/funk/mesh.c)
- DESYNC (Degesys, Rose, Patel, Nagpal; IPSN 2007): nodes **anti-synchronise** periodic transmissions into an evenly spaced round-robin, giving a TDMA schedule with no global clock that adapts to node count. On Telos motes under high contention, message loss dropped from **about 58% to under 1%** with 25% more throughput than the default MAC — [Harvard TR-18-06](https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstream/handle/1/25680331/tr-18-06.pdf?sequence=1); [IPSN 2007 record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/desync-self-organizing-desynchronization-and-tdma-on-wireless-sen/). It is single-hop by design, and multi-hop needs extra mechanisms such as a refractory threshold for stale information — [Mühlberger et al. FGSN 2012](https://informatik.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/1003-ti/user_upload/staff/muehlberger/On_the_Improvement_of_a_Self-Organized_MAC_Protocol_for_Multi_Hop_Wireless_Sensor_Networks__FGSN2012_.pdf)

**BLE advertising at density (if BLE adverts are considered instead of ESP-NOW)**
- Collision probability rises with the number of advertisers. The capture effect recovered on average 2.95% (up to 7.8% at 14 devices) in tests, and up to 25% PDR in dense simulations — [IMT Atlantique capture effect study](https://hal-imt-atlantique.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03165168)
- Periodic BLE adverts repeat collisions more than ALOHA predicts, and the simple access mechanism causes significant loss as deployments grow — [ReliaBLE (UC Berkeley)](https://www.eecs.berkeley.edu/~prabal/pubs/papers/zachariah22reliable.pdf). In BLE Mesh, too many relays hurt delivery past a point — [Bluetooth Mesh performance (arXiv 1910.03345)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.03345)

**RSSI proximity in crowds**
- Leith & Farrell (TCD, 2020): BLE RSSI varies substantially with handset orientation, **absorption by the human body**, and reflections in buildings and trains. RSSI "need not decrease with increasing distance", and body and orientation effects are comparable to moving 2 m — [arXiv 2006.06822](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.06822)
- In a Dublin commuter bus study (GAEN API, 60 location pairs), **changing only the people holding the handsets changed attenuation by about ±10 dB**. The Swiss app's rule detected zero exposures, although all pairs were within 2 m for 15 min or more. The best alternative threshold reached 5–8% detection — [arXiv 2006.08543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08543)
- Body loss at 2.4 GHz (anechoic, patch antennas, IEEE 802.15 contribution): with a person 0.3 m from the receiver on a 3 m link, median loss is **17 dB facing front and 11 dB side-on**, worst case (5σ) 34 dB and 21 dB. At 5 m it is 13 and 8 dB. A person mid-link gives about 5–10 dB — [IEEE 802.15-08-0434](https://mentor.ieee.org/802.15/dcn/08/15-08-0434-00-0006-path-loss-due-to-human-body.pdf)
- In an open-plan office, people's presence and movement raised mean path loss up to 4 dB, with up to 9 dB variation at high activity — [HAL open-office study](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01870223)

**Better ranging than RSSI**
- Wi-Fi FTM (802.11mc) on ESP32-S3, HopFTM (TU Berlin, LCN 2025): taking the minimum RTT across 4 channels cut median error from **120 cm to 30 cm** and worst case from 15 m to 10 m. In a simulated mobile case it went from 1.8 m to 0.76 m. Multipath makes FTM overestimate distance — [HopFTM paper](https://www.tkn.tu-berlin.de/bib/roesler2025improving/roesler2025improving.pdf). Off-the-shelf FTM often has multi-metre errors — [Wi-PRO](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/wi-pro-accurate-indoor-pnt-using-ftm-with-multipath-suppression/)
- BLE Channel Sounding on nRF54L15 DKs (user reports, 2025): about 0.5–1 m error at 0–6 m with boards face-to-face, and much worse in other setups (more than 4 m error at 4.5 m). Nordic: IFFT method has about 50 cm usable resolution and RTT about 5 m, "no guarantees for accuracy just yet". The Bluetooth SIG markets ±50 cm — [Nordic DevZone thread 124901](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/124901/channel-sounding-distance-accurancy-issue/551396); [Nordic DevZone thread 123554](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/123554/channel-sounding-on-nrf54l15dk-accuracy-of-ifft-value/545061)
- Espressif: the ESP32-E22 (announced Jan 2026) lists BLE 5.4, not 6.0. ESP-IDF's feature-status table shows Channel Sounding rows as unsupported in the version seen — [Espressif ESP32-E22 announcement](https://www.espressif.com/en/news/ESP32_E22_Announcement); [ESP-IDF BLE feature support status](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/release-v5.2/esp32/api-guides/ble/ble-feature-support-status.html)

### Inferences
- **Airtime estimate (my calculation, not from a source).** Take an ESP-NOW broadcast at the default 1 Mbps with a 32-byte body. Adding about 43 bytes of 802.11 MAC header, ESP-NOW vendor header and FCS gives about 75 bytes, so about 600 µs on air plus about 192 µs DSSS preamble. With DIFS and average backoff that is roughly 1.0–1.2 ms per frame, or an upper bound of roughly 800–1,000 frames/s per channel per collision domain before collisions. 300 badges × 20 Hz = 6,000 frames/s, about 6–7× over that ceiling. At 3 Hz (900 frames/s) it is at the ceiling. At 1 Hz (300 frames/s) it is about a third, leaving room for effects and camp Wi-Fi. A higher OFDM rate set via `esp_now_set_peer_rate_config` (e.g. 6 Mbps) cuts airtime per frame roughly 4×, at the cost of range and sensitivity. Outdoors, line-of-sight ESP-NOW reaches 100–150 m+ (WLED suit report above), so a whole camp village would likely be one collision domain.
- Recommended combination from the prior art:
  1. A base station (or several, PixMob-style) broadcasts `{epoch/generation, heartbeat phase or BPM, timebase_ms, active effect IDs}` at 1–5 Hz. Badges render locally from the timebase (WLED, `espnow_time`).
  2. Badges broadcast at most about 1 Hz with random jitter (r0ket 0.5–1.5×), with Trickle-style suppression: skip your slot if you've already heard k≥2–3 identical up-to-date beacons this interval.
  3. Effects ("virus", "wave") spread as state records `{effect_id, origin, seq/timestamp, ttl, start_time}`, deduplicated by ID or newest-wins (r0ket, esp-now `magic`). Use a small TTL (≤3, as in Meshtastic event mode) and an RSSI floor so only strong-signal neighbours relay (esp-now `forward_rssi`).
  4. Without a base station, fall back to CrowdClock highest-clock adoption with a bounded step size, or a reachback-firefly PCO.
  5. Add a global "nice"/rate knob in the beacon (r0ket) so organisers can throttle the swarm live.
- RSSI is worth using only as a coarse "near / not near" with heavy averaging and hysteresis. Body loss (10–30 dB) dwarfs the roughly 6 dB per doubling of distance in free space. In a dense crowd, a badge worn on the chest is shadowed by its own wearer in half the directions. For "close encounter" effects, a short-range physical channel (IR as on the 39C3 badge, NFMI as on DC27, or deliberately minimal TX power) gives more predictable behaviour than RSSI thresholds.
- FTM and Channel Sounding are pairwise and session-based. Ranging hundreds of neighbours continuously would cost far more airtime than the heartbeat broadcast, so these are suitable only for occasional one-to-one interactions, not swarm-wide proximity.
- ESPHome is an awkward fit for a several-hundred-node swarm. Its ESP-NOW component is peer-oriented (20-peer limit, `on_broadcast` only for registered peers, one `on_unknown_peer` hook) and lacks encryption. A custom component or plain Arduino/ESP-IDF code would be needed for open broadcast gossip.

### Gaps
- No published measurement of ESP-NOW broadcast loss with more than 50 nodes on one channel was found. The largest found was 8 nodes (Espressif blog). A staged test (10/25/50/100 nodes, sequence-number loss accounting) would be needed.
- I found no ESP-NOW-specific measurement of time-sync accuracy (µs-level). Jitter tails of 5–100 ms are reported anecdotally.
- No ESP32 has built-in UWB. That is general knowledge, and I found no Espressif UWB product or ESP32-C6 UWB feature in these searches. External UWB modules were not researched.
- The exact Wi-Fi channel and coexistence plan of the Camp 2023/2027 NOC (which 2.4 GHz channels are used for AP coverage) was not found.

### Inferences
- Across these sources the recurring failure modes are rarely raw radio physics. They are: (a) one node or bridge flooding the channel (Hamvention 2024); (b) stack defaults (BLE scan filters, duplicate filtering); (c) dependence on event infrastructure (camp Wi-Fi portal, OTA); (d) late hardware or battery-safety issues (WHY2025, Disobey 2025, DC27 prototype-to-production); (e) spoofing on shared-key broadcast channels (Meshtastic). Herz-Schwarm should plan for a rogue or modded badge sending fake "virus" or epoch-reset packets. ESP-NOW broadcast cannot be encrypted, so authenticity needs an app-level MAC (e.g. a truncated HMAC on base-station packets).
- Battery data for always-listening 2.4 GHz wearables was almost absent from these badge sources. PixMob (4–7 h) and Xylobands (8–12 h claimed) are receive-only. The ESP-NOW wake-window API is the documented lever for duty-cycling RX, and it only works if the heartbeat beacon timing is predictable. That is another argument for a slotted, low-rate beacon.

### Gaps
- No post-mortem found for: Supercon 2025 LoRa mesh, AND!XOR DC26 ESP32 botnet at scale, Aramcon nRF52840 mesh (beyond "no audio"), r0ket mesh at Camp 2011, card10 BLE at camp.
- No measured battery-life numbers for any always-on ESP32 radio badge at a camp were found.
