# Device Mesh and Swarm Networking: What Scales to Hundreds of Battery Badges at a Hacker Camp

## Protocol Landscape: Meshtastic, MeshCore, and the Alternatives

The LoRa mesh space has consolidated around three stacks with distinct philosophies. **Meshtastic** uses managed flooding — every node rebroadcasts with hop limits and deduplication, making it ideal for ad-hoc, roaming populations like a camp where nodes come and go ([Meshtastic](https://meshtastic.org/), [Meshtastic Firmware Repository](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware)). **MeshCore** uses structured routing (directed flooding with repeater roles), trading ad-hoc flexibility for lower overhead and better behavior in fixed-infrastructure deployments ([MeshCore: Lightweight LoRa Mesh Networking Library](https://github.com/meshcore-dev/MeshCore)). **Reticulum** is a full network stack rather than a chat app, now portable to MCUs via microReticulum, a C++ port with TLSF allocators for memory-constrained devices ([microReticulum - Reticulum Network Stack for MCUs](https://github.com/attermann/microReticulum)). Comparison guides consistently frame it as: Meshtastic for roaming users, MeshCore for planned infrastructure, Reticulum for general-purpose off-grid networking ([Meshtastic vs MeshCore vs Reticulum Comparison](https://simeononsecurity.com/articles/meshtastic-vs-meshcore-vs-reticulum-comparison-guide/), [MeshCore vs Meshtastic Comparison Guide](https://simeononsecurity.com/articles/meshcore-vs-meshtastic-comparison-guide/)).

For badge-to-badge links at camp scale, the short-range options matter more than LoRa:

- **ESP-NOW** (Espressif's connectionless 802.11 protocol) is the workhorse: no association overhead, ~250-byte payloads, one-to-many and many-to-many support ([ESP-NOW Component](https://github.com/espressif/esp-now)). Espressif's own comparison table puts ESP-NOW against BLE Mesh, Thread, ESP-Mesh-Lite, and Zigbee on node count, distance, throughput, and latency ([Comparison of Espressif Mesh Solutions](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-techpedia/en/latest/esp-friends/solution-introduction/mesh/mesh-comparison.html)).
- **Mesh libraries on top of ESP-NOW**: Mesh-NOW adds automatic peer discovery, multi-hop routing, and optional AES-128-GCM encryption across five ESP32 variants ([Mesh-NOW: ESP32 Mesh Networking Library](https://github.com/NellowTCS/Mesh-NOW)); zh_network offers broadcast/unicast mesh for ESP-IDF and ESP8266 RTOS ([zh_network ESP-NOW Mesh Network Component](https://github.com/aZholtikov/zh_network)).
- **painlessMesh** builds self-organizing Wi-Fi meshes on ESP8266/ESP32 with JSON messaging and no central controller — simple, but Wi-Fi-based meshes cost battery life ([painlessMesh: Mesh Networking for ESP8266/ESP32](https://github.com/gmag11/painlessMesh)).
- **ESP-Mesh-Lite** is Espressif's SoftAP+Station Wi-Fi tree mesh; unlike ESP-MESH it lets sub-devices reach the internet independently, but it's gateway-oriented rather than peer-to-peer ([ESP-Mesh-Lite Wi-Fi Mesh Networking Solution](https://github.com/espressif/esp-mesh-lite)).
- **BLE** is the historical badge-to-badge medium (r0ket's 2.4 GHz radio, card10, Fri3d), and NimBLE-Arduino dramatically cuts RAM/flash footprint versus Bluedroid for ESP32 BLE apps ([NimBLE-Arduino BLE Library](https://github.com/h2zero/nimble-arduino)).
- **IEEE 802.15.4** is available directly on ESP32-C6/H2 via an ESP-IDF component with promiscuous mode, RSSI/LQI callbacks, and channel switching — an underused option for dense badge swarms ([IEEE 802.15.4 Transceiver ESP-IDF Component](https://github.com/shoderico/ieee802154_transceiver)).

**Airtime is the fundamental constraint.** LoRa's duty-cycle limits (1% in EU 868 MHz) mean a shared channel saturates quickly with hundreds of chatty nodes — this is why Meshtastic aggressively rate-limits telemetry and position broadcasts. ESP-NOW/BLE have no regulatory duty cycle but contend for the same 2.4 GHz spectrum as everyone's phones and Wi-Fi at camp.

## Radio Hardware and Boards

The dominant LoRa transceiver family is now **Semtech SX126x** (SX1261/62/68, LLCC68), supported across ESPHome, RadioLib, and virtually all current Meshtastic/MeshCore hardware ([ESPHome SX126x Transceiver Component Documentation](https://esphome.io/components/sx126x/)). **RadioLib** is the universal Arduino driver layer underneath most projects — one API for LoRa, FSK, RTTY, SSTV across Semtech, HopeRF, and Nordic modules ([RadioLib: Universal Wireless Library for Embedded Devices](https://github.com/jgromes/RadioLib)).

Concrete board options:

- **Heltec WiFi LoRa 32 V4** — ESP32-S3 + SX1262, explicitly compatible with Meshtastic, MeshCore, and LoRaWAN ([WiFi LoRa 32 V4 Development Board](https://heltec.org/project/wifi-lora-32-v4/)).
- **Heltec Mesh Node T114** — nRF52840 + SX1262 with 1.14" TFT; the nRF52 family is the battery-life champion due to deep-sleep currents in the single-digit µA range, which is why Meshtastic firmware targets it alongside ESP32 and RP2040/RP2350 ([Mesh Node T114 LoRa Node Product Page](https://heltec.org/project/mesh-node-t114/), [Meshtastic Firmware Repository](https://github.com/meshtastic/firmware)).
- **RAK3312 WisBlock** — ESP32-S3 + SX1262 combined module for compact builds ([RAK3312 WisBlock Core Module for LoRaWAN](https://store.rakwireless.com/products/wisblock-core-module-rak3312-lora-wifi-ble)).
- Commercial ecosystems exist for both stacks: Solid Link Systems sells nodes, antennas, cases, and solar kits ([Solid Link Systems](https://solidlinksystems.com/)).

For badge hardware specifically, the pattern across a decade of event badges is: **ESP32 (now S3/C6) or RP2350 as MCU, a small LiPo cell, and either BLE or a LoRa radio added via SPI**. The Badgeware family (Badger/Tufty/Blinky) standardizes on RP2350 with WiFi, BLE, USB-C, and a 1000 mAh battery ([The squad — Badger, Tufty, and Blinky](https://badgewa.re/)). A general ESP32 buyer's guide covers the S2/S3/C3 tradeoffs relevant to badge design ([ESP32 Buyer's Guide: Different Chips, Firmware, Sensors](https://eitherway.io/posts/esp32-buyers-guide/)).

## Deployed Event Badges: Lessons from the Field

This is where the topic has the richest evidence.

**Meshtastic at DEF CON 2025** is the largest documented deployment: **over 2,000 nodes** forming the biggest known Meshtastic network. It also produced the most instructive security finding: a NodeInfo spoofing attack exploiting the Trust-on-First-Use model and memory constraints, patched post-event ([Meshtastic DEF CON 2025 Security Findings](https://meshtastic.org/blog/that-one-time-at-defcon/)). The lesson for badge meshes: identity binding and memory limits become attack surfaces exactly when density is highest.

**WHY2025** (Dutch outdoor hacker camp, August 2025) is the canonical badge-mesh case study — and a cautionary tale:

- The badge shipped without its LoRa radio wired up; getting MeshCore running required **soldering bodge wires between the radio and ESP32-C6 and attaching an 868 MHz antenna**, plus replacing 18650 holders with pouch cells ([WHY2025 Badge MeshCore Guide](https://peetz0r.nl/badge), [MeshCore on WHY2025 Badge Session](https://events.ccc.de/congress/2025/hub/en/event/detail/meshcore-on-your-why2025-badge)). Firmware lives at [MeshCore WHY2025 Badge Firmware](https://codeberg.org/Peetz0r/meshcore-why2025-badge); an independent Meshtastic port required compiling custom firmware for both ESP32-C6 and C4 ([Installing Meshtastic on WHY2025 Badge](https://hackgyver.fr/pages/writeups/why2025-meshtastic), [Meshtastic Firmware Repository](https://github.com/n0p/mesthastic-fw-why-badge/tree/why2025-badge)).
- The badge was formally declared a **fire hazard**: exposed copper traces, inadequate solder mask, and cell-holder design created short-circuit risks with unprotected 18650 cells ([WHY2025 Badge Fire Hazard Advisory](https://wiki.why2025.org/Badge/Fire_hazard)). Battery mechanical design is a first-class engineering problem, not an afterthought.

**Hackaday Supercon 2025 Communicator Badge** is the newest entry: ESP32-S3 with 8 MB PSRAM/16 MB flash, dome-switch keyboard, LCD, and **LoRa mesh networking as the badge's core feature** — a sign that mesh messaging is now a headline badge capability rather than a hack ([2025 Hackaday Supercon Communicator Badge Reveal](https://hackaday.com/2025/10/23/announcing-the-2025-hackaday-superconference-communicator-badge/), [2025 Hackaday Supercon Communicator Badge](https://github.com/Hack-a-Day/2025-Communicator_Badge)).

Historical lineage: **r0ket** (Camp 2011) pioneered the ARM Cortex-M3 + 2.4 GHz radio + unbrickable bootloader + m0dul expansion-socket pattern that later badges copied ([r0ket Electronic Name Tag Concept](https://r0ket.badge.events.ccc.de/concept)). **card10** (Camp 2019) shipped BLE with pairing management and companion apps ([card10 BLE User Guide](https://card10.badge.events.ccc.de/userguide/ble/)). **flow3r** (Camp 2023) was an ESP32-S3 instrument badge, open hardware/firmware, 32 EUR add-on ([Camp 2023 – The flow3r Badge](https://events.ccc.de/en/2023/06/05/camp23-the-flow3r-badge/)). **Tildagon** (EMF 2024) went modular: hexagonal hub with six "hexpansion" ports, making the badge reusable across events ([EMF 2024 Tildagon Badge Overview](https://hackaday.com/2024/03/23/the-electromagnetic-field-2024-badge-is-a-little-different/)). **Fri3d Friends** demonstrated the contact-swap use case: BLE discovery of nearby badges in shared groups, contact exchange on button press, on MicroPython-based ESP32 badges ([Fri3d Friends: Badge Contact Swap App](https://steeman.be/posts/building-fri3d-friends/)).

The **Da Bomb DEF CON indie badge post-mortem** remains the best checklist of production failure modes: scheduling slips, component sourcing, PCB assembly delays, an SAO I2C bus conflict, and distribution problems that left some customers unserved ([Da Bomb Badge Post Mortem](https://hackaday.com/2019/09/02/after-the-con-da-bomb-badge-post-mortem/)).

## Firmware and Toolchain Support

Toolchain maturity is uneven across the stacks:

- **Arduino/PlatformIO**: RadioLib and NimBLE-Arduino are the mature foundations ([RadioLib](https://github.com/jgromes/RadioLib), [NimBLE-Arduino](https://github.com/h2zero/nimble-arduino)). painlessMesh and Mesh-NOW are Arduino/ESP-IDF friendly.
- **ESPHome** has quietly become a viable badge-firmware platform: the ESPNow component handles channels, peers, and broadcast automations ([ESPHome ESPNow Communication Component](https://esphome.io/components/espnow/)); the SX126x component drives LoRa transceivers directly ([ESPHome SX126x](https://esphome.io/components/sx126x/)); and the **packet_transport** abstraction unifies ESP-NOW, SX126x/SX127x, UART, and UDP with optional encryption and rolling codes for node-to-node messaging without a broker ([ESPHome Packet Transport Component](https://esphome.io/components/packet_transport/)).
- **WLED** shows the broadcast-sync pattern at scale: its UDP notifier syncs colors, brightness, and effects across devices with sync groups and a versioned packet protocol — directly relevant to synchronized LED badge swarms ([WLED UDP Sync Protocol Documentation](https://kno.wled.ge/interfaces/udp-notifier/), [wled/WLED](https://github.com/wled/WLED)).
- **Meshtasticator** fills the pre-deployment gap: a discrete-event simulator of Meshtastic's radio layer for performance/scalability analysis, plus an interactive simulator ([Meshtasticator: Meshtastic Network Simulator](https://github.com/GUVWAF/Meshtasticator)). **STRIDEtastic** provides passive monitoring/observability for Meshtastic networks via MQTT/serial/TCP ingestion ([0wulf/stridetastic](https://github.com/0wulf/stridetastic)).

## Scaling Problems: Time Sync, Airtime, and Congestion

The research literature offers directly applicable techniques for dense badge swarms:

- **Trickle (RFC 6206)** is the canonical density-aware rate limiter: transmit quickly when information is new, exponentially back off when consistent, scaling logarithmically with density — ideal for badge firmware-update or state-propagation traffic ([RFC 6206: The Trickle Algorithm](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6206)).
- **Firefly-inspired synchronization (RFA)** achieves decentralized phase sync on real motes with realistic radio effects — the natural model for making hundreds of LED badges blink in unison ([Firefly-Inspired Sensor Network Synchronicity with Realistic Radio Effects](https://ssr.princeton.edu/publications/firefly-inspired-sensor-network-synchronicity-realistic-radio-effects)).
- **DESYNC** does the inverse: self-organizing desynchronization gives round-robin TDMA without a global clock, with convergence proofs and Telos-mote experiments — a way to divide airtime among hundreds of badges without coordination ([DESYNC: Self-organizing desynchronization and TDMA on wireless sensor networks](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/desync-self-organizing-desynchronization-and-tdma-on-wireless-sen/)).

Empirical range/reliability data:

- **ESP-NOW outdoor testing** (ESP32-C6, open field and forest) across Wi-Fi, ESP-NOW, and ESP-NOW-LR found Wi-Fi best for high-speed short range and ESP-NOW variants superior at distance, with detailed latency/speed/packet-success-rate curves ([ESP32 Wireless Protocols Outdoor Comparison](https://developer.espressif.com/blog/esp-now-for-outdoor-applications/)).
- **ESP-NOW indoor reliability** with 8 sensor nodes feeding a central node produced concrete packet-loss numbers and the recommendation to segment buildings into zones — relevant to indoor conference venues ([ESP-NOW Indoor Reliability Study](https://developer.espressif.com/blog/reliability-esp-now/)).
- **Meshtastic in practice**: rural/coastal deployments achieve surprising long range, while suburban RF noise and terrain sharply limit performance ([Meshtastic Mesh Network Urban vs Rural Experience](https://hackaday.com/2025/10/09/meshtastic-a-tale-of-two-cities/)). **MeshCore** first-hand reviews report frustrating onboarding, limited urban range, and surprisingly closed-source clients ([MeshCore Off-Grid Messaging First Impressions](https://mtlynch.io/first-impressions-of-meshcore/), [MeshCore Off-Grid Messaging: Lessons Learned](https://hackaday.com/2025/12/06/lessons-learned-after-trying-meshcore-for-off-grid-text-messaging/)).

## Practical Guidance and Open Questions

**Choosing a stack by scale and purpose:**

- **Hundreds of badges, LED sync and badge-to-badge interaction at a camp**: ESP-NOW or BLE, with Trickle-style rate limiting and firefly/DESYNC-inspired scheduling. LoRa's airtime budget cannot absorb hundreds of chatty nodes; DEF CON's 2,000-node Meshtastic network worked because most nodes were mostly listening.
- **Camp-wide messaging layer**: LoRa mesh (Meshtastic for roaming users, MeshCore if you deploy fixed repeaters), with telemetry and position broadcasts rate-limited hard.
- **Battery life**: nRF52 for multi-week LoRa nodes; ESP32 variants are fine for badges if you design sleep around the event duration and accept daily charging. Solar works even in Alaska with garden-light panels for fixed Meshtastic nodes ([Solar Powered Meshtastic Practicality](https://hackaday.com/2025/09/17/the-practicality-of-solar-powered-meshtastic/)).

**Open questions and gaps:**

- No source documents a *successful* pre-planned mesh of hundreds of battery badges — WHY2025's mesh was a post-hoc bodge-wire rescue, and DEF CON's was organic. Designing the radio in from the start (as Supercon 2025 appears to do) is the emerging lesson.
- Tooling for predicting airtime congestion at badge-swarm density is thin; Meshtasticator exists for Meshtastic only, and nothing simulates ESP-NOW swarms at camp scale.
- Security at density remains unsolved: TOFU identity, NodeInfo spoofing, and memory exhaustion all surfaced at DEF CON 2025.
- Battery mechanical safety (WHY2025 fire hazard) and production logistics (Da Bomb) are recurring, avoidable failure modes.

One source sits at the topic's edge: Elli Furedy's talk on the Neotropolis LARP "Portable Data Node" is about emergent swarm *design philosophy* — people before tech, embedding meaning in devices — rather than networking itself, but its principle that badge swarms succeed as social objects before technical ones matches the deployment record ([Elli Furedy: Sandbox Systems for Emergent Games](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ndodsA254HA)).
