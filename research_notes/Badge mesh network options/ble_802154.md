# Bluetooth LE (incl. Coded PHY, periodic adv, mesh) and IEEE 802.15.4 (Thread, Zigbee, raw) for the Herz-Schwarm badge swarm

Scope: a broadcast-heavy swarm of a few hundred ESP32-class LED badges (today: 17-byte ESP-NOW packet at 20 Hz per badge, RSSI proximity, base-station effect injection, Wi-Fi RX ~90 mA always on). Research date: 2026-10-17. Datasheet figures are vendor "typical/peak" values at 3.3 V / 25 °C unless noted. "Inference" items are my own arithmetic or reasoning from the cited numbers, not measured.

Reference airtime numbers used throughout (my derivation from the packet formats; see section 1 for the sources of the timing rules):
- Legacy non-connectable BLE advert (ADV_NONCONN_IND, 1M PHY) carrying the 17-byte payload as manufacturer data: 1 B preamble + 4 B access address + 2 B header + 6 B AdvA + 21 B AD (len+type+company ID+17) + 3 B CRC = 37 B, about **0.30 ms per channel**, about **0.9 ms per advertising event** (3 primary channels).
- Extended advertising with the AUX packet on 2M PHY: ADV_EXT_IND (1M, ~17 B, ~0.14 ms) x 3 + AUX_ADV_IND (2M, ~42 B, ~0.17 ms), about **0.6 ms per event**. 2M cannot be used on the primary channels, so it saves little for small payloads.
- Extended advertising on LE Coded S=8: ADV_EXT_IND ~1.17 ms x 3 + AUX_ADV_IND ~2.7 ms, about **6.2 ms per event**, roughly **7x the legacy 1M airtime**.
- Raw IEEE 802.15.4 frame (250 kbit/s, 32 µs/byte): 6 B SHR+PHR + 9 B MAC header (short addresses, PAN-ID compression) + 17 B payload + 2 B FCS = 34 B, about **1.09 ms**, plus CSMA-CA backoff (not counted).

## 1. BLE advertising as a connectionless broadcast swarm: capacity, PHYs, intervals, scanner duty cycle, beat sync (periodic advertising, PAwR)

### Takeaway
Connectionless BLE advertising is ALOHA-like: nothing coordinates when advertisers transmit, so the collision rate grows with (number of badges in range) x (rate) x (airtime). At the current 20 Hz per badge, legacy 1M adverts start to break down at around 50 to 100 badges within mutual radio range. Coded PHY S=8 breaks down at only about 10 to 25 badges, because it uses about 7x the airtime. A few hundred badges in one radio cell works only if the per-badge broadcast rate falls to about 1 to 5 Hz, with the 20 Hz beat kept by each badge's local clock. Periodic advertising and PAwR (Bluetooth 5.4) give a scheduled, TDMA-like structure that scales to thousands of receivers of one broadcaster. But PAwR is only "experimental" on ESP32-C6 and "unsupported" on ESP32-S3, and it gives no badge-to-badge RSSI.

### Cited Findings
- Ruuvi collision model: two packets are lost if they overlap. P(hit) between two advertisers = 2 x T_tx / T_interval, and the chance of missing all others = P(miss)^(N-1). With a measured ~650 µs on-air time (ramp-up to ramp-down; theoretical 376 µs for a full 376-bit advert) at a 1 s interval, P(hit) = 0.13 % per pair. Peak modelled throughput was ~769 transmissions/s, with about two-thirds of packets lost. — [Ruuvi: How many Bluetooth beacons can I have in one place?](https://ruuvi.com/?p=2908)
- Ruuvi measurements: 46 beacons at a 100 ms interval were heard reliably (an overnight test of 165,600 samples had 70 missing, so a 10 s detection window was 99.94 % reliable). A measured 100 ms interval averaged 105 ms because of the random advertising delay. A refined model matching 1 to 16 beacons used 450 µs airtime and 92 % gateway loss, meaning the receiver, not collisions, was the main loss. Extrapolation (not verified): 460 beacons at 1 s heard within ~100 s. — [Ruuvi](https://ruuvi.com/?p=2908)
- Nordic engineer's rule of thumb (theory, no experiment at that scale): 500 beacons at a 1 s interval gives about 50 % packet collisions, and with 1000 beacons "most" packets are lost. — [Nordic DevZone: scalability of the scan function](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/14926/scalability-of-the-scan-function)
- Large real deployment (Nikodem & Bawiec 2019): more than 200 advertising tags into one hub. The advertisement collision rate was 0.22 to 0.33, but repeated advertisements still gave acceptable data reception. — [Experimental Evaluation of Advertisement-Based BLE Communication (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6983080/)
- Capture effect: measured with 2 to 14 advertisers in a Faraday box and simulated up to 1000 advertisers. Capture raises the packet delivery ratio by up to 25 % in dense simulations, and by +2.95 % on average (peak 7.8 %) with 14 real devices. Pure collision models are therefore somewhat pessimistic. — [HAL: Be aware of the capture effect](https://hal-imt-atlantique.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03165168)
- BLE 5 system-level simulation (open office): the coded PHYs performed worse as the network became congested. — [Aalto: system-level performance evaluation of Bluetooth 5 in IoT](https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/items/acb42652-667f-4ceb-9594-04224fe76683)
- Concurrent-transmission study: the 125 kbit/s coded PHY benefits from "narrow beating" when several transmitters send the same packet, but loses that advantage for long packets. The 500 kbit/s coded PHY did best in very noisy environments. — [arXiv 2005.13816: Impact of the Physical Layer on Concurrent Transmissions](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.13816)
- Coded PHY timing: the symbol rate stays at 1 Msym/s, and each bit becomes 8 symbols at S=8 ("every bit takes 8x longer"). Preamble ~80 µs, access address ~256 µs, CI 16 µs, TERM1/TERM2 24 µs each are always sent at S=8. — [Hubble: How BLE Coded PHY S=8 achieves 4x range at the cost of 8x airtime](https://hubble.com/community/guides/how-ble-coded-phy-s-8-achieves-4x-range-at-the-cost-of-8x-airtime/)
- Long-range advertising sends a short coded packet on 37/38/39 that points to a larger packet on a data channel. The scanner has to receive both, and the longer coded packets face a higher risk of interference. — [Nordic DevZone: advertising Coded S8 range thread](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/58496/advertising-phy-coded-s8-long-range-stops-at-nearly-20m-built/237414)
- Extended advertising moves the payload off the three primary channels onto the 37 data channels, which the Bluetooth SIG presents as improving scalability and collision resistance. — [Bluetooth SIG: Bluetooth 5 overview (2019)](https://www.bluetooth.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Bluetooth_5-FINAL.pdf)
- ESPHome's beacon component allows a 20 to 10240 ms advertising interval, so 50 ms (20 Hz) is within the legal range. — [ESPHome esp32_ble_beacon](https://esphome.io/components/esp32_ble_beacon/)
- Scanner duty cycle: in ESPHome's tracker the scan `window` is the time actually listening and `interval` is the scan period (default 320 ms). RX power scales with window/interval. — [ESPHome esp32_ble_tracker](https://esphome.io/components/esp32_ble_tracker/)
- PAwR (Bluetooth 5.4): the central keeps a periodic train. Each periodic interval has 1 to 128 subevents with 0 to 255 response slots each, and peripherals synchronise and reply in assigned slots without a connection. — [Silicon Labs: Bluetooth ESL](https://docs.silabs.com/bluetooth/10.1.0/bluetooth-electronic-shelf-label/04-bluetooth-esl)
- PAwR scale: the protocol maximum is quoted as 32,640 devices. — [CNX Software: Bluetooth 5.4 adds ESL support](https://cnx-software.com/?p=109699). Silicon Labs' worked example is 10,000 ESLs with ~3 s latency, using 50 subevents x 200 response slots, a 3 s interval, ~0.75 ms per slot and 1M PHY. — [Silicon Labs BT-203: Everything you need to know about Bluetooth 5.4](https://www.silabs.com/documents/public/presentations/bt-203-everything-you-need-to-know-about-bluetooth-5-4.pdf)
- PAwR observers only listen briefly at predefined times, which is the energy benefit. — [ST wiki: PAwR-ESL features](https://wiki.st.com/stm32mcu/wiki/Connectivity:Bluetooth_LE_PAwR-ESL_features_and_applications)
- ESP-IDF status for ESP32-C6 (latest docs): LE Advertising Extensions, Coded PHY S=2/S=8, 2M and Periodic Advertising Sync Transfer are *supported*. PAwR, LE Power Control and Advertising Coding Selection are *experimental*. Isochronous channels are *unsupported*. — [ESP-IDF BLE feature support status, ESP32-C6](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/latest/esp32c6/api-guides/ble/ble-feature-support-status.html)
- ESP32-S3: Coded PHY, 2M and extended advertising are supported. Periodic Advertising Sync Transfer and PAwR are *unsupported*. — [ESP-IDF BLE feature support status, ESP32-S3](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/latest/esp32s3/api-guides/ble/ble-feature-support-status.html)
- Periodic sync on ESP32: an Espressif Bluedroid "periodic_sync" example lists ESP32-C3 and ESP32-S3 as targets — [ESP-IDF example README (mirror)](https://codex.ac6.fr/Zephyr/xref/hal_espressif-3.6.0/examples/bluetooth/bluedroid/ble_50/peroidic_sync/README.md). An Espressif component example says "NimBLE does not support periodic sync yet. Try using Bluedroid" — [Espressif ble_conn_mgr ble_ext_scan example](https://components.espressif.com/components/espressif/ble_conn_mgr/versions/1.3.1/examples/ble_conn_mgr/ble_ext_scan). These conflict with NimBLE-Arduino's own docs, which list periodic advertising (see section 2). Support probably differs by version.
- Zephyr: `CONFIG_BT_PER_ADV_SYNC_MAX` (simultaneous periodic advertising syncs) has range 1 to 64 and default 1 (Zephyr 2.7 docs, older). — [Zephyr Kconfig CONFIG_BT_PER_ADV_SYNC_MAX](https://docs.zephyrproject.org/2.7.5/reference/kconfig/CONFIG_BT_PER_ADV_SYNC_MAX.html)

### Inferences
- **Collision arithmetic for the swarm** (Ruuvi pairwise model, no capture, 100 % scanner duty, all badges in mutual range). Per-pair hit probability is p = 2T/interval, and the share of a given badge's packets that one scanner receives is (1-p)^(N-1):
  - Legacy 1M, T ≈ 0.30 ms, 20 Hz (50 ms): p ≈ 1.2 %. N = 25 → ~75 % received; N = 50 → ~56 %; N = 100 → ~31 %; N = 300 → ~3 %.
  - Legacy 1M at 5 Hz: N = 100 → ~75 %; N = 300 → ~41 %. At 1 Hz: N = 300 → ~84 %; N = 1000 → ~55 %.
  - Coded S=8, primary-channel packet T ≈ 1.17 ms, 20 Hz: p ≈ 4.7 %. N = 10 → ~65 %; N = 25 → ~32 %; N = 50 → ~10 %. At 1 Hz: N = 100 → ~79 %; N = 300 → ~50 %. The AUX packet adds more loss, although it is spread over 37 data channels.
  - Channel-occupancy ceiling, if transmissions were perfectly scheduled: one 0.30 ms legacy advert at 20 Hz fills a primary channel at about 170 badges. Random access tops out well below that.
- **Rate is the lever, not PHY.** The beat is a phase counter. If every badge free-runs the 20 Hz beat on its own crystal and only corrects phase from received packets, the radio rate can drop to 1 to 5 Hz. That gives roughly a 4 to 20x capacity gain, with the same factor saved in TX energy. Typical crystals are tens of ppm (not sourced here), so drift over a few seconds stays far below an LED frame time.
- **Coded PHY is a poor fit for a dense crowd.** Its +7 to 8 dB link budget (section 2) roughly doubles free-space range, so a 2 to 4x larger area falls into one collision domain, while each packet costs ~7x more airtime. For a proximity/RSSI effect (metres to tens of metres), 1M legacy advertising at low TX power is the better fit. Coded PHY makes sense only for a sparse long-range "base station to badges" downlink.
- **2M PHY gains little.** For a 17-byte payload, the 1M primary-channel ADV_EXT_IND overhead remains, so the event airtime falls only from ~0.9 to ~0.6 ms, and scanners must follow the AuxPtr.
- **Beat sync options.** (a) Base station runs periodic advertising, or PAwR on ESP32-C6/H2/nRF. Badges sync and wake only for the scheduled events, which gives very low RX duty and deterministic latency (the interval). This suits the beat, effect IDs and the wave start time. (b) Badges still need some scanning of each other's adverts for RSSI proximity. That can be done at a low duty cycle (for example a 10 % window), because proximity does not need every packet. (c) PAwR response slots could carry per-badge status back to the base, but standard controllers do not report other badges' responses to a listening badge, so badge-to-badge RSSI through PAwR is not available out of the box. This point is my reading of how PAwR works and is not verified.
- One base station covers one radio cell only. Several hundred metres of camp would need several periodic or PAwR broadcasters, or badges re-broadcasting the beat (flooding), which brings the collision problem back.

### Gaps
- I found no published measurement of dense *extended*-advertising or Coded-PHY swarms (packet reception rate vs node count). The capacity numbers above are model-based.
- I did not find the maximum number of simultaneous periodic-advertising syncs supported by the ESP-IDF NimBLE/ESP controller (the Kconfig name was not confirmed).
- I did not find ESP-IDF PAwR example maturity or a measured PAwR current on ESP32 parts.

## 2. Chip and stack support for Coded PHY / extended advertising, and measured Coded PHY range

### Takeaway
All current Espressif BLE 5 parts (ESP32-C3, S3, C6, H2, C5) have Coded PHY and extended advertising in ESP-IDF. The classic ESP32 has neither: every BLE 5.0 feature row is "unsupported". On Arduino the practical route is NimBLE-Arduino with `CONFIG_BT_NIMBLE_EXT_ADV`, but forum reports show Coded PHY can be fiddly on ESP32-S3 and C3 (S=2 advertising missing on S3, one C3 user unable to enable Coded). Measured Coded PHY range varies from ~170 m (custom nRF52840 boards, 0 dBm) to 1300 m (Nordic DKs, line of sight). The nRF54L15 has no Arduino IDE support, only Zephyr through PlatformIO or nRF Connect SDK.

### Cited Findings
- Classic ESP32: Coded PHY, 2M PHY, LE Advertising Extensions, PAST, PAwR and Channel Selection Algorithm #2 are all *unsupported* on the controller, Bluedroid and NimBLE. Only 4.2-level features are supported. — [ESP-IDF BLE feature support status, ESP32](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/latest/esp32/api-guides/ble/ble-feature-support-status.html)
- ESP32-S3: Coded PHY, 2M and extended advertising are supported on the controller and both hosts. PAST, PAwR and ISO are unsupported. — [ESP-IDF, ESP32-S3](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/latest/esp32s3/api-guides/ble/ble-feature-support-status.html)
- ESP32-C6: Coded PHY (S=2/S=8), 2M, advertising extensions and PAST are supported. PAwR is experimental. Advertiser Monitor is "developing (expected by 2026/12)". — [ESP-IDF, ESP32-C6](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/latest/esp32c6/api-guides/ble/ble-feature-support-status.html)
- Datasheets list Coded PHY (125/500 kbit/s) for ESP32-S3 (v2.2), ESP32-C6 (v1.5), ESP32-H2 (v1.3) and ESP32-C5 (v1.5, BLE 5 + 802.15.4 + dual-band Wi-Fi 6). — [ESP32-S3 datasheet](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32-s3_datasheet_en.pdf), [ESP32-C6 datasheet](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32-c6_datasheet_en.pdf), [ESP32-H2 datasheet](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32-h2_datasheet_en.pdf), [ESP32-C5 datasheet](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32-c5_datasheet_en.pdf)

- Two nRF52840 DKs, Coded PHY, 8 dBm: a connection held to 300 m (after rain) and 400 m (clear day). — [Nordic DevZone: nRF52840 long range test](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/60121/nrf52840-long-range-test)

- nRF54L15 boards: the Seeed XIAO nRF54L15 is supported by nRF Connect SDK, Zephyr and PlatformIO (`framework = zephyr`, Seeed platform repo). The Arduino IDE is "not currently supported". ### Inferences
- Coded S=8 adds about 7 dB of sensitivity over 1M on every Espressif part (e.g. −97.5 → −104.5 dBm on S3, −99 → −106.5 on H2). In free space that is about 2.2x range (20·log10). The 1300 m vs 655 m Nordic result is consistent with this.
- The ESP32-H2 has the best Espressif sensitivity on every PHY, by 1 to 1.5 dB over S3/C3. The differences between Espressif chips are small compared with antenna and body effects.
- On a chest-worn badge with a small PCB antenna and 10 to 17 dB body loss (section 7), expect tens to a couple of hundred metres on Coded, not kilometres.
- For Arduino/PlatformIO, ESP32-S3/C3/C6/H2 with NimBLE-Arduino ext-adv is the realistic path. The nRF54L15 needs Zephyr, which is available under PlatformIO but has no Arduino API.

### Gaps
- No measured Coded PHY range on small ESP32-C3/S3/C6/H2 boards was found.
- I did not research the nRF52840 Arduino cores (e.g. Adafruit nRF52/Bluefruit) for extended advertising or Coded PHY.
- The number of concurrent advertising sets on Espressif controllers was not stated on the feature-status pages.

## 3. BLE Mesh (ESP-BLE-MESH, Zephyr Bluetooth Mesh) for 20 Hz beat sync vs occasional effect commands

### Takeaway
BLE Mesh is managed flooding over the same advertising channels, so every relay multiplies airtime. It is documented as being prone to broadcast storms in dense deployments, with application throughput below 1 kbit/s (Espressif) and about 100 nodes in Espressif's internal tests. It cannot carry a 20 Hz all-to-all beat. It could carry occasional effect commands (one message per few seconds, TTL-limited) from a base, but a dedicated simple flood or periodic broadcast does the same with less overhead. Low Power Node/Friend polling adds latency and is not designed for sub-second sync.

### Cited Findings
- ESP-BLE-MESH supports Relay, Proxy, Low Power and Friend features and is certified against Mesh Profile v1.0.1. The docs give no node-count or latency figures. — [ESP-IDF ESP-BLE-MESH docs](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v5.5.3/esp32h2/api-guides/esp-ble-mesh/ble-mesh-index.html)
- Espressif's mesh comparison (internal tests): BLE Mesh theoretical max 32,767 nodes, ~100 nodes tested with an ESP32-S3 gateway; single-hop latency <100 ms at 50 m and <50 ms below 50 m; throughput <1 kbit/s; recommended adjacent-node spacing ≤50 m. Node chips listed are ESP32, ESP32-C3 and ESP32-H2. — [Espressif techpedia: Comparison of Different Mesh Solutions](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-techpedia/en/latest/esp-friends/solution-introduction/mesh/mesh-comparison.html)
- Bluetooth Mesh simulation study (Hernández-Solana et al., arXiv 2019): average delay ~250 ms for more than 10 hops under the worst simulated conditions. Scalability is "especially challenging" because of broadcast storms. Randomising timing parameters and varying the advertising-event duration reduce flooding drawbacks. — [arXiv 1910.03345](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03345)
- Same paper: with 52, 68, 86 and 106 devices, packet loss improved from 52 to 86 devices, but at 106 devices "loss rates start increasing again". Too many relays hinder delivery, and delay rises with density. They used a random backoff of up to 20 ms. — [arXiv 1910.03345 (PDF)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.03345)
- Same paper: Bluetooth Mesh is vulnerable to WLAN interference even though it uses only the 3 advertising channels (office measurements). — [arXiv 1910.03345](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03345)
- Independent ESP-BLE-MESH experiments (HAW Hamburg bachelor thesis) measured PDR vs distance and two-hop networks with more sources and relays. Different factors limit performance depending on traffic direction. — [HAW Hamburg thesis: Performance evaluation of BLE Mesh network](https://reposit.haw-hamburg.de/bitstream/20.500.12738/17265/1/BA_Performance_evaluation_of_BLEM_network_geschw%c3%a4rzt.pdf)
- Anecdote (May 2026): slow forwarding through an ESP32-C6 GATT proxy into the mesh. — [esp32.com thread](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?p=154569)
- On ESP32-C6, BLE Mesh combined with Wi-Fi needs manual coexistence status calls (`esp_coex_status_bit_set/clear`). — [ESP-IDF RF coexistence, ESP32-C6](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/latest/esp32c6/api-guides/coexist.html)

### Inferences
- In mesh, each of R relays in range re-transmits every message. A 300-badge mesh at 20 Hz would multiply the already saturated advertising load (section 1) by R, which is impossible. Even at 1 Hz with relaying, the load sits at the edge of the collision regime.
- Mesh is reasonable for base-station effect commands at ≤1 message per several seconds with a small TTL. A simpler custom approach — a base broadcast that badges re-broadcast once, with a sequence number and random delay — gives the same coverage without provisioning, keys and model overhead. My judgement, not tested.
- Low Power Node/Friend polling suits sensors, not a badge that needs sub-100 ms beat alignment. A periodic-advertising sync (section 1) is the low-power listening mechanism that fits this use case.

### Gaps
- I found no published ESP-BLE-MESH or Zephyr mesh measurement with hundreds of nodes, and no measured LPN/Friend current or poll latency on ESP32/nRF parts.

## 4. Radio currents (RX/TX/sleep) and realistic average current for a mostly-listening badge

### Takeaway
On the ESP32-S3, BLE RX (93 mA) costs *more* than Wi-Fi RX (88 mA). Moving the existing S3 badge from ESP-NOW to BLE gives no power saving unless the receiver is duty-cycled. ESP32-C6/C5 BLE and 802.15.4 RX (71 to 79 mA) are only ~10 to 20 % below S3 Wi-Fi. The ESP32-H2 (24 to 25 mA RX) is ~3.7x better, and Nordic parts (nRF52840 ~4.6 to 6.3 mA, nRF54L15 ~3.2 to 3.4 mA RX) are 15 to 25x better. The 64 WS2812 LEDs idle at ~26 to 64 mA even when dark, so once the radio drops to H2/Nordic levels the LED matrix and its rail switching dominate battery life.

### Cited Findings

- **Continuous listening** (RX on 100 %, plus ~4 to 10 mA for the CPU running animations, taken from the datasheet difference between modem-sleep "running" and "idle"):
  - S3 BLE ≈ 95 to 110 mA (no better than today's Wi-Fi).
  - C6 ≈ 75 to 80 mA.
  - C5 ≈ 80 to 85 mA.
  - H2 ≈ 28 to 32 mA.
  - nRF52840 ≈ 6 to 9 mA.
  - nRF54L15 ≈ 4 to 6 mA.
- **TX share at 20 Hz** with a 0.9 ms legacy event: S3 at 0 dBm ≈ 3.2 mA, H2 at 0 dBm ≈ 0.65 mA, nRF52840 ≈ 0.1 mA. With Coded S=8 (6.2 ms per event), H2 at 0 dBm ≈ 4.5 mA at 20 Hz but only ≈ 0.2 mA at 1 Hz.
- **Duty-cycled listening** — e.g. synced to a base periodic train plus a 10 % scan window for RSSI: H2 radio ≈ 2.5 to 3 mA, nRF52840 ≈ 0.5 to 1 mA. The CPU and LEDs then dominate.
- **LED floor:** 64 WS2812 x 0.4 to 1 mA = 26 to 64 mA quiescent. Example (assumed 1000 mAh cell, ignoring LED light current):
  - Today, S3 Wi-Fi ~95 + LEDs 26 to 64 ≈ 120 to 160 mA → ~6 to 8 h.
  - H2 continuous RX ~30 + LEDs ≈ 56 to 94 mA → ~11 to 18 h.
  - nRF duty-cycled ~2 + LEDs ≈ 28 to 66 mA → ~15 to 35 h.

  "Much better battery life" therefore needs both a low-RX-current radio (H2/nRF) and LED power management (rail switching, lower duty, or lower-quiescent LEDs). The radio change alone gives about 1.5 to 2x.
- Switching an S3 badge to BLE without changing the silicon brings capacity and toolchain changes but **no** power benefit, unless the protocol is made schedule-based so the receiver sleeps.

### Gaps
- No independent measured BLE-scan average current was found for ESP32-C6, H2 or S3 with light sleep enabled. The datasheet RX values are "peak" figures measured with the CPU idle.
- ESP32-C3 BLE current is not listed in the C3 datasheet (v2.4).
- I found no measured light-sleep wake-up overhead, which matters for fine-grained duty cycling.

## 5. IEEE 802.15.4: Thread, Zigbee, raw 802.15.4 broadcast; Wi-Fi + 802.15.4 coexistence on ESP32-C6

### Takeaway
Thread and Zigbee are routed networks built for sparse sensor and actuator traffic. Thread is limited to 32 routers (511 children each), Espressif measured 4 to 5 KB/s UDP, and multicast floods through MPL. In Zigbee, broadcasts are throttled by the broadcast transaction table to roughly 1 per second network-wide in Digi's implementation. Neither can carry 300 badges x 20 Hz of state. Raw 802.15.4 broadcast is technically possible on ESP32-C6/H2/C5 through the ESP-IDF `ieee802154` driver, but there is no Arduino wrapper and only thin documentation. At 250 kbit/s a 17-byte frame takes ~1.1 ms, about 3.6x a legacy BLE advert, so one channel saturates at about 46 badges at 20 Hz. It is a plausible ESP-NOW replacement only at much lower rates or with time-slotting, and on C6/C5 it saves no power (RX 74 to 78 mA). On ESP32-C6, Wi-Fi + 802.15.4 coexistence is time-division, and 802.15.4 normal RX has the lowest priority, so "ESP-NOW + 802.15.4 in parallel" is not a sound plan.

### Cited Findings
- Thread: one Leader, up to 32 routers, up to 511 end devices per router (theoretical 16,384). Thread keeps 16 to 23 routers, and the theoretical maximum has not been tested in the real world. — [OpenThread: Node roles and types](https://openthread.io/guides/thread-primer/node-roles-and-types)
- The practical Thread device count is bounded by router RAM (child tables, buffers for sleepy children). — [Nordic DevZone: max nodes in Thread star topology](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/45194/maximum-amount-of-nodes-in-thread-star-topology/178062)
- Espressif internal tests: Thread 300 nodes tested, average single-hop latency 10 ms, UDP ~8 KB/s theoretical and 4 to 5 KB/s measured, ~200 m link with ESP32-H2 (−102.5 dBm RX, 20 dBm TX). Zigbee 300 nodes stable, average single hop 16 ms, ~16.9 kbit/s application rate, ~300 m range under the same H2 conditions. — [Espressif techpedia mesh comparison](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-techpedia/en/latest/esp-friends/solution-introduction/mesh/mesh-comparison.html)
- 802.15.4 RX sensitivity at 250 kbit/s (1 % PER): ESP32-C6 −104 dBm, ESP32-C5 −104 dBm, ESP32-H2 −102.5 dBm. TX up to 20 dBm. — [ESP32-C6 datasheet](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32-c6_datasheet_en.pdf), [ESP32-C5 datasheet](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32-c5_datasheet_en.pdf), [ESP32-H2 datasheet](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32-h2_datasheet_en.pdf)
- Zigbee broadcast transaction table: in Digi's stack each entry lives 8 s and the table holds 8 entries, "effectively limiting network broadcast transmissions to once per second". — [Digi: Broadcast transmissions](https://docs.digi.com/resources/documentation/digidocs/90001539/concepts/c_broadcast_transmissions.htm). A TI user saw broadcasts dropped in sets of 9, and BTT size and lifetime are stack-specific. — [TI E2E: timing issues in multicast and broadcast](https://e2e.ti.com/support/wireless-connectivity/zigbee-thread-group/zigbee-and-thread/f/zigbee-thread-forum/316404/timing-issues-in-multicast-and-broadcast). See also [ns-3 Zigbee BroadcastTransactionTable](https://www.nsnam.org/doxygen/db/d2c/classns3_1_1zigbee_1_1_broadcast_transaction_table.html).
- ESP-IDF raw driver: `components/ieee802154/include/esp_ieee802154.h` declares enable, channel, power, PAN ID/address, promiscuous mode, `esp_ieee802154_transmit(frame, cca)` and receive functions. — [esp_ieee802154.h (SourceVu mirror)](https://sourcevu.sysprogs.com/espressif/esp-idf/files/components/ieee802154/include/esp_ieee802154.h). Since IDF v5.1.3 the application must call `esp_ieee802154_receive_handle_done()` after handling each received frame, or the buffer is not freed. — [ESP-IDF 5.2 migration guide: IEEE 802.15.4](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v5.4/esp32c6/migration-guides/release-5.x/5.2/ieee802154.html). The receive-done callback type is marked "only used within ESP-IDF". — [SourceVu: esp_ieee802154_receive_done_cb_t](https://sourcevu.sysprogs.com/espressif/esp-idf/symbols/esp_ieee802154_receive_done_cb_t)
- ESP-IDF ships `examples/ieee802154/ieee802154_cli`, which "runs on an 802.15.4 SoC like ESP32-H2". — [ESP-IDF examples/ieee802154](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/tree/master/examples/ieee802154)
- Community ESP-IDF component `shoderico/ieee802154_transceiver` wraps raw TX/RX (IDF ≥5.0, tested 5.4.1) — [GitHub](https://github.com/shoderico/ieee802154_transceiver). Espressif's Rust `esp-ieee802154` crate (C6/H2) has `transmit_raw` — [esp-ieee802154 source](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/rust/esp-ieee802154/0.8.0/src/esp_ieee802154/lib.rs.html).
- Arduino: arduino-esp32 has a Zigbee wrapper (ESP-Zigbee-SDK/ZBOSS) for C6/H2 with all Zigbee roles (Aug 2024) — [CNX Software](https://cnx-software.com/2024/08/23/arduino-core-for-esp32-gets-a-zigbee-wrapper-library). It also has an OpenThread library (`OThread`, `OThreadCLI`, `DataSet`) — [arduino-esp32 OpenThread docs](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/arduino-esp32/en/latest/openthread/openthread.html). Native OpenThread UDP sockets arrived via PRs #12672/#12646 per a 2026 forum thread — [esp32.com](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?p=154996). A Matter-over-Thread example targets Arduino 3.3.2+ / IDF 5.5.1+ — [Espressif component registry](https://components.espressif.com/components/espressif/arduino-esp32/versions/3.3.6/examples/Arduino_ESP_Matter_over_OpenThread). I found no raw 802.15.4 Arduino API.
- ESP32-C6 coexistence: one 2.4 GHz RF shared by time-division. Wi-Fi STA connected + Thread/Zigbee router = C1 ("supported, performance unstable"); STA + end device = Y; SoftAP + router = X (not supported). Thread/Zigbee router or scan + BLE scan = X; end device + BLE scan = C1. ESP-NOW is **not listed** in the Wi-Fi/802.15.4 table. Normal 802.15.4 receive has the **lowest** priority, and Wi-Fi/BLE traffic can increase Thread/Zigbee packet loss. Espressif recommends a dual-SoC design (e.g. ESP32-S3 + ESP32-H2, separate antennas) for Wi-Fi gateways. ESP-NOW RX + BLE = S (stable in STA mode only). — [ESP-IDF RF coexistence, ESP32-C6](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/latest/esp32c6/api-guides/coexist.html)
- The ESP32-C5 datasheet describes "time-division coexistence of Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and 802.15.4". — [ESP32-C5 datasheet v1.5](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32-c5_datasheet_en.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Thread/Zigbee capacity vs need:** 300 badges x 20 Hz x 17 B ≈ 102 KB/s of payload alone, about 20x Espressif's measured 4 to 5 KB/s Thread UDP throughput. Even at 1 Hz (≈5 KB/s) it would saturate a single Thread partition, and Thread multicast is flooded through routers by MPL. Zigbee's ~1 broadcast/s budget rules it out entirely. Both are acceptable for occasional base-station effect commands to a few hundred nodes, but they need commissioning and a leader/coordinator, and each joins on a single channel.
- **Raw 802.15.4 broadcast:** 34 B ≈ 1.09 ms per frame, so a channel is 100 % busy at ~46 badges x 20 Hz, and CSMA-CA saturates earlier. In the pairwise model at 20 Hz without CSMA, 25 badges in range deliver ~34 % and 50 deliver ~11 %. At 1 Hz, about 900 frames/s would fill a channel. Raw 802.15.4 therefore beats BLE legacy advertising only when (a) the 16 channels are used to partition groups, or (b) a TDMA beacon schedule is built on top (the driver supports timed TX/RX per the coexistence page's "timed transmit/receive" priority note). It is a credible ESP-NOW replacement on ESP32-H2 for power reasons (25 mA RX). On C6/C5 it is power-neutral compared with Wi-Fi RX.
- **Toolchain risk:** a raw 802.15.4 swarm means ESP-IDF code (usable from PlatformIO with the `espidf` framework, or Arduino-as-IDF-component) against a lightly documented driver API. There is no Arduino or ESPHome abstraction.
- **ESP32-C6 for "Wi-Fi for base and OTA, 802.15.4 for swarm"** works only in a time-shared way. 802.15.4 RX gets whatever airtime Wi-Fi/BLE leave, which conflicts with continuous swarm listening.

### Gaps
- I found no measured Thread MPL (realm-local multicast) latency or delivery numbers for 100+ nodes.
- I found no published raw-802.15.4 swarm or broadcast capacity measurement on ESP32-C6/H2.
- Whether ESP-NOW + 802.15.4 coexistence works at all on C6/C5 is not stated in the official coexistence table.

## 6. ESPHome support (state as of late 2026): BLE, Thread, Zigbee, nRF52 — what a custom swarm protocol can and cannot do

### Takeaway
ESPHome supports BLE beaconing (iBeacon only), scanning (interval/window), BLE server/client and proxy; OpenThread on ESP32-C5/C6/H2/H4/H21/S31 (ESP-IDF only) and nRF52840 (Zephyr); an nRF52 (Zephyr) platform since 2025.8; and a Zigbee end device on nRF52 since 2026.1 (ESP32 Zigbee was an open PR). None of the BLE components document extended advertising, Coded PHY, periodic advertising or raw 802.15.4. A custom swarm protocol inside ESPHome means writing a C++ external component that calls ESP-IDF/NimBLE/ieee802154 APIs directly, which leaves ESPHome as mostly an OTA/config shell.

## 7. Interference at a hacker camp; BLE channel hopping vs fixed Wi-Fi/802.15.4 channels; body blocking

### Takeaway
BLE advertising uses three fixed channels (2402/2426/2480 MHz) placed between Wi-Fi channels 1/6/11, but modern Wi-Fi deployments, and CCC-style 1/5/9/13 channel plans, overlap them. BLE adverts get frequency diversity (each event is sent on all three channels), while ESP-NOW and 802.15.4 sit on one fixed channel. Extended advertising puts the payload on the 37 hopping data channels. The human body costs ~10 to 17 dB median at 2.4 GHz (up to ~34 dB worst-case), which is larger than the Coded PHY gain, so chest-worn badges will see strongly orientation-dependent RSSI with any 2.4 GHz radio.

### Cited Findings
- BLE advertising channels 37/38/39 are 2402, 2426 and 2480 MHz, chosen not to collide with Wi-Fi channels 1, 6 and 11. — [SemFio Networks: BLE advertisement channels](https://semfionetworks.com/blog/ble-advertisment-channels). Wi-Fi's much higher power can still cause coexistence issues — [TI SWRA475A coexistence note](https://www.ti.com/document-viewer/lit/html/SWRA475A/coexistence-swra4755506) — and real Wi-Fi often runs on channels like 3, 4 or 9 — [Hubble: BLE coexistence](https://hubble.com/community/guides/ble-coexistence-when-wifi-and-bluetooth-fight/).
- 802.15.4 has 16 channels (11 to 26, 5 MHz spacing, 2 MHz wide). Channels 15, 20, 25 and 26 are least affected by Wi-Fi 1/6/11. — [arXiv 2502.13303: cross-technology interference in low-power networks](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.13303). Channels 25 and 26 need reduced TX power under FCC rules. — [Digi: Zigbee channels](https://docs.digi.com/resources/documentation/digidocs/90001537/references/r_channels_zigbee.htm)
- Bluetooth Mesh traffic, limited to the 3 advertising channels, was vulnerable to measured office WLAN interference. — [arXiv 1910.03345](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03345)
- In a dense coded-PHY test the poster attributed range loss to "many nearby Wi-Fi devices". — [Nordic DevZone 49926](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/49926/nrf52840-coded-phy-range-problem)
- CCC context: Chaos Communication Camp 2023 had ~6000 participants (Mildenberg, 15 to 19 Aug 2023) — [Wikipedia: Chaos Communication Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaos_Communication_Camp). The 37C3 (winter congress, Dec 2023) network plan used 2.4 GHz channels 1, 5, 9 and 13 at 20 MHz with 802.11b disabled. — [37C3 network info](https://events.ccc.de/congress/2023/infos/network.html). I found no camp-specific 2.4 GHz plan or statistics.
- Body shadowing at 2.4 GHz (IEEE 802.15 contribution, anechoic chamber, patch antennas): median body loss ~17 dB with the body facing the link at 0.3 m and ~11 dB side-on; ~10/9 dB at 1.5 m; 5σ tail up to 34 dB. — [IEEE 802.15-08-0434: path loss due to human body](https://mentor.ieee.org/802.15/dcn/08/15-08-0434-00-0006-path-loss-due-to-human-body.pdf). On-body waist-to-chest links measure ~51 to 61 dB path loss, and antennas detune close to the body. — [IEICE Trans. Commun. E91-B(6)](https://global.ieice.org/en_transactions/communications/10.1093/ietcom/e91-b.6.1682/_pdf)

### Inferences
- With a 1/5/9/13 Wi-Fi plan (as at 37C3), the "BLE advertising avoids Wi-Fi" argument largely fails. Wi-Fi centres are 2412, 2432, 2452 and 2472 MHz, and I assumed ~16.6 MHz of occupied OFDM bandwidth (not sourced here). BLE ch 38 (2426 MHz) then lies inside Wi-Fi ch 5, BLE ch 39 (2480 MHz) sits on the upper edge of ch 13, and BLE ch 37 (2402 MHz) lies just below ch 1's occupied band. Only ~4 MHz gaps remain between the four Wi-Fi channels. 802.15.4 ch 15 (2425) and ch 20 (2450) fall inside Wi-Fi ch 5 and ch 9, ch 25 (2475) inside ch 13, and ch 26 (2480) on ch 13's edge, the same spot as BLE ch 39. Under such a plan there is no clean 2.4 GHz channel for a fixed-channel swarm. The actual camp plan should be checked with the NOC.
- BLE's three-channel advertising gives each event three independent chances against narrowband interference. ESP-NOW and raw 802.15.4 on one channel have none unless the protocol hops. Extended advertising's AUX packets use channel selection across 37 data channels, which spreads collisions but makes the scanner follow pointers.
- Body loss (10 to 17 dB median) is larger than the Coded PHY gain (~7 dB). For RSSI-based proximity, body orientation will add ±10 dB noise, so averaging over many packets matters more than PHY choice. That argues for more frequent but cheap packets, i.e. legacy 1M, over Coded PHY.
- Other badge projects, phones, BLE trackers and drones at a camp also use the BLE advertising channels. The pairwise collision estimates in section 1 are optimistic because they ignore this foreign traffic.

### Gaps
- No measured 2.4 GHz spectrum occupancy or BLE advertising-channel load at a CCC camp was found, and no 2023 camp Wi-Fi channel plan.
- No measurement was found specific to chest-worn badges with small PCB antennas (badge-to-badge loss through two bodies).
