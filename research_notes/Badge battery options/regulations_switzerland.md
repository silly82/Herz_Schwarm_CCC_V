# Swiss battery rules, disposal and customs for the battery badge (state: October 2026)

Source: primary texts on fedlex.admin.ch, bafu.admin.ch, inobat.ch, bazg.admin.ch, estv.admin.ch, gesetze-im-internet.de, umweltbundesamt.de and eur-lex.europa.eu, read 17–18 October 2026. Secondary sources are named as such. Claims that could not be checked are marked **unverified**. This is not legal advice.

Scope: what Swiss law asks of a team that imports about 10, 100 or 1000 LiPo cells (103030, about 18–20 g and 3.3–3.7 Wh each), builds them into badges in Switzerland, and takes most of the badges to Germany. Covered here: Swiss labelling, substance limits and the advance disposal fee (VEG); Swiss rule changes; disposal of spent and damaged cells in CH and DE; customs and VAT both ways. EU and German producer duties, transport and event rules are in `regulations.md`.

## 1. ChemRRV Annex 2.15 "Batterien", the advance disposal fee (VEG) and INOBAT

### Takeaway
- The Chemical Risk Reduction Ordinance (ChemRRV, SR 814.81) bans batteries with more than 5 mg/kg mercury. It also bans portable batteries with more than 20 mg/kg cadmium.
- It requires a separate-collection mark on each battery, and Hg/Cd/Pb symbols above set limits. A rechargeable portable battery must also show its capacity.
- **Switzerland has no rule that batteries in devices be removable.**
- The VEG is owed by the "Herstellerin", and goes to INOBAT. The Herstellerin is whoever makes the batteries or devices containing them, or **imports them "beruflich oder gewerblich"** (in the course of a profession or business). Nothing more is owed if the fee was already paid.
- INOBAT's 2026 tariff is **CHF 0.05 per Li-ion cell of 1–49 g**, excluding VAT. For 1000 cells that is about CHF 50 plus 8.1 % VAT.
- Cells bought from a Swiss distributor already carry the fee. A direct import puts the duty on the importer.
- Since 1 July 2024, the fee can be refunded on exported batteries, minus costs already incurred.
- **Unverified:** whether a non-commercial hobby team counts as a fee-liable Herstellerin at all. Ask INOBAT: 031 380 79 30, info@inobat.ch.

### Cited findings
- **Version.** SR 814.81 is consolidated as of 16 July 2026. Annex 2.15 was last changed by the ordinance of 31 May 2024, in force since 1 July 2024 (AS 2024 254) — [ChemRRV Annex 2.15](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2005/478/de#annex_2_15). The future consolidation of 1 January 2027 already on fedlex has the same Annex 2.15.
- **Who is the Herstellerin.**
  - ChemRRV Art. 2 lit. a: anyone who makes, extracts or imports goods "beruflich oder gewerblich".
  - The Chemicals Act (ChemG, SR 813.1) Art. 4 Abs. 1 lit. i defines "Inverkehrbringen" (placing on the market). It means making goods available or handing them to third parties, and includes "Einfuhr zu beruflichen oder gewerblichen Zwecken" — [ChemG](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2004/724/de).
- **Definitions (Ziff. 1).** A "Gerätebatterie" (portable battery) is a sealed battery that can be carried by hand and is not meant only for industrial use. The badge's 103030 cell is one.
- **Substances (Ziff. 2).**
  - Mercury above 5 mg/kg is banned in all batteries, including batteries in devices.
  - Cadmium above 20 mg/kg is banned in portable batteries.
  - Lead is not banned. Above 40 mg/kg it only triggers the "Pb" mark.
- **Labelling (Ziff. 4.1).**
  - The maker of a battery, or of a device containing one, must ensure a visible, legible and durable separate-collection mark on the battery. Its form is that of Art. 21 of Directive 2006/66/EC.
  - Art. 21(3): the crossed-out bin covers at least 3 % of the battery's largest side, at most 5 × 5 cm. Art. 21(5): if that comes out smaller than 0.5 × 0.5 cm, a 1 × 1 cm symbol goes on the packaging instead — [Directive 2006/66/EC, consolidated](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02006L0066-20180704).
  - Calculation: 3 % of 30 × 30 mm is about 0.52 × 0.52 cm, so the mark belongs on the cell itself.
  - Rechargeable portable batteries must show their capacity, as in Regulation (EU) 1103/2010 (Ziff. 4.1 Abs. 3, 5).
- **Removability.** Annex 2.15 has no removability or replaceability rule; the full text was read. Neither does the e-waste ordinance VREG (SR 814.620) — [VREG](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2021/633/de).
- **Sales and take-back.**
  - Ziff. 4.2: shops must display that batteries have to be returned, are taken back free of charge and carry a fee.
  - Ziff. 5.2 Abs. 1: anyone who sells portable batteries must take them back free of charge at every sales point.
  - Ziff. 5.2 Abs. 3: manufacturers take back the battery types they supply.
  - Ziff. 5.1: consumers must return batteries.
- **Fee duty (Ziff. 6.1).**
  - Abs. 1: the fee is paid to the organisation mandated by BAFU, which is INOBAT. It is owed by (a) battery manufacturers, and (b) manufacturers of devices containing batteries, "wenn diese Batterien nicht bereits mit der Gebühr belastet sind".
  - Abs. 2: a third party may take over the fee and the reporting.
- **Fee level.**
  - Ziff. 6.2 sets the legal range: CHF 0.10–7.00 per kg, at least CHF 0.03 per battery.
  - The UVEK ordinance SR 814.670.1 (as of 1 September 2023) sets CHF 1.60/kg for Li-ion portable batteries, at least CHF 0.03 each — [SR 814.670.1](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2011/876/de).
  - INOBAT's tariff valid from 1 January 2026, article group 62000 (Li-ion and other rechargeables), excluding VAT: 62001 (1–24 g) CHF 0.05; 62002 (25–49 g) CHF 0.05; 62003 (50–74 g) CHF 0.10.
  - The weight reported is the bare cell, or the cell with its housing when the two cannot be separated.
  - For batteries in devices reported through the e-waste schemes SENS or SWICO, the VEG is paid automatically (group 70000; Li-ion in electronics at CHF 1.60/kg) — [INOBAT tariff 2026](https://www.inobat.ch/jwa/vfs/web/inobat.ch/media/recyclingpartner/hersteller-importeure/gebuehren-beitraege/Geb%C3%BChrentarif%20alle%20Batterien_01%2001%202026_DE.pdf).
- **Reporting.**
  - Ziff. 6.3: reporting is monthly unless agreed otherwise. Ziff. 6.4: INOBAT invoices, payable within 30 days.
  - In practice: register online, then send a quarterly self-declaration by 15 April, 15 July, 15 October and 15 January. Half-yearly or yearly reporting can be agreed.
  - **A nil report is required even in quarters without imports.** Without self-declarations, INOBAT estimates from customs (BAZG) data and adds fees — [INOBAT, Hersteller/Importeure](https://www.inobat.ch/de/recyclingpartner/hersteller-importeure). The reminder fee is CHF 50 at the second reminder — [Meldefristen und Mahngebühren](https://www.inobat.ch/jwa/vfs/web/inobat.ch/media/recyclingpartner/hersteller-importeure/gebuehren-beitraege/Meldefristen-und-Mahngebuehren-DE.pdf).
  - No published threshold or exemption for small or private importers was found.
- **Customs data.** Ziff. 6.7 Abs. 5: BAZG may pass import declarations to INOBAT. Abs. 6 allows the fee to be collected at import; there is no sign that this is in use.
- **Export refund (Ziff. 6.6bis, since 1 July 2024).**
  - Whoever exports batteries can claim a refund on a reasoned request, minus costs already incurred. The deadline is 31 March of the following year, and INOBAT decides by formal ruling (6.9).
  - The explanatory report of 31 May 2024 proposed a CHF 100 processing fee for portable batteries — [explanatory report](https://www.newsd.admin.ch/newsd/message/attachments/87955.pdf). That fee is not in the consolidated fee ordinance SR 813.153.1 as of 1 January 2026 (**unverified** whether it applies). If it does, a refund on CHF 50 of fees is not worth claiming.
- **The badge as an electronic device (VREG).** A manufacturer is whoever imports "zur gewerblichen Abgabe" (Art. 3 lit. c). The device carries the crossed-out bin (Art. 4) and is taken back free of charge (Art. 6). This mostly matters if badges are handed out in Switzerland.
- **BAFU's overview** (23 May 2024): "Die VEG ist im Kaufpreis inbegriffen" — [BAFU, Batterien](https://www.bafu.admin.ch/de/batterien).

## 2. Changes from 2025 to 2027, and the EU Battery Regulation

### Takeaway
- No Swiss draft adopts the EU Battery Regulation (EU) 2023/1542. Swiss rules still point to the repealed Directive 2006/66/EC.
- The last battery change entered into force on 1 July 2024: the export refund and reporting details.
- BAFU says it is checking whether changes are needed, with no timetable.
- The revised Environmental Protection Act (USG), in force since 1 January 2025, lets the Federal Council extend the VEG to foreign online sellers. The ChemRRV does not do so yet.
- Badges handed out in Germany fall under the EU regulation; see `regulations.md`.

### Cited findings
- **BAFU** is examining "ob rechtliche Anpassungen nötig sind" (whether legal changes are needed) in view of the EU regulation — [BAFU, Batterien](https://www.bafu.admin.ch/de/batterien), [English page](https://www.bafu.admin.ch/en/batteries).
- **Environmental ordinance packages.**
  - The autumn 2026 package was consulted from 22 December 2025 to 12 April 2026 (fedlex `proj/2025/123`). Its ChemRRV drafts touch Annexes 1.1, 1.7, 1.16, 2.5, 2.6, 2.9, 2.10, 2.18 and 2.19. **None touches Annex 2.15**, and the drafts never mention "Batter" — [consultation](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/dl/proj/2025/123/cons_1/de).
  - The same package lets recognised industry organisations oblige non-members, including foreign online sellers, to pay recycling contributions; this is relevant to the SENS and SWICO e-waste schemes.
  - The spring 2026 package covers packaging and waste, not batteries — [Swiss Recycle's reply](https://swissrecycle.ch/fileadmin/user_upload/VP_19_Vernehmlassungsantwort_Swiss_Recycle.pdf).
- **USG** (as of 1 August 2026; changes in force since 1 January 2025, AS 2024 648) — [USG](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1984/1122_1122_1122/de):
  - Art. 32abis Abs. 1/1bis allows the VEG to be imposed on foreign online mail-order companies.
  - Abs. 4 sends customs data to INOBAT.
  - Abs. 5 excludes imports of fee-liable products from the simplified customs declaration. What this means for postal or courier imports of cells is **unverified**.
  - Arts. 32aquater–32asepties add a domestic representative, information duties for platforms, and import bans or seizure for non-payers.
- **Parliament.**
  - The Federal Council's reply to 24.4406 says the implementing ordinances are being drafted — [24.4406](https://www.parlament.ch/de/ratsbetrieb/suche-curia-vista/geschaeft?AffairId=20244406).
  - Its reply to 25.4252 says Art. 32ater is not yet in force — [25.4252](https://www.parlament.ch/de/ratsbetrieb/suche-curia-vista/geschaeft?AffairId=20254252).
  - No motion to adopt the EU Battery Regulation was found.
- **EU dates relevant to badges taken to Germany** — [Regulation (EU) 2023/1542](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1542):
  - **Art. 11, removability and replaceability, "shall apply from 18 February 2027".** The end user must be able to remove the battery with commercially available tools, without thermal energy or solvents. Instructions must come with the product and stay online.
  - A JST plug with the cell held by tape probably meets this. Strong adhesive might not.
  - Art. 13 labels apply from 18 August 2026, and the QR code from 18 February 2027.

## 3. Disposal of spent and damaged LiPo cells

### Takeaway
- **Switzerland.** Intact cells go free of charge to any shop that sells batteries (more than 11,000 points) or to a municipal collection point. Recycling is done by Batrec in Wimmis.
- Damaged or swollen cells go **only to staffed collection points**, terminals taped and the cell bagged.
- INOBAT runs a free 24/7 emergency service for hot or critical Li-ion batteries. It serves households, municipal collection points, small firms with up to 5 staff, and emergency services.
- **Germany.** Retailers take back the battery types they sell, and municipal recycling centres take all household portable batteries, both free of charge under the BattDG.
- For damaged cells in Germany: tape the terminals, use a sealed container with sand, and tell the staff. Collection points ship damaged cells under GRS's "red" safety standard.

### Cited findings
- **INOBAT, Li-ion page.**
  - Damaged or swollen batteries may be handed in only at a staffed collection point.
  - Tape the terminals, use a separate plastic bag, and don't remove built-in batteries: return the whole device.
  - A Li-ion recycling line has run at Batrec Industrie AG since the end of November 2023 — [INOBAT, Lithium-Ionen-Akkus](https://www.inobat.ch/de/batterierecycling/lithium-ionen-akkus).
- **INOBAT, collection.** Take-back is free at all sales points "unabhängig von einem Kauf", at more than 11,000 collection points, plus municipal ones — [INOBAT, Sammlung](https://www.inobat.ch/de/batterierecycling/sammlung-entsorgung).
- **INOBAT emergency service.**
  - Staffed 24/7 and free for Li-ion batteries on which the fee was paid.
  - Partners: Altola 062 287 23 83, Sovag Veolia 041 429 70 15, Chiresa 058 400 07 00 — [INOBAT, Notfallorganisation](https://www.inobat.ch/de/recyclingpartner/notfallorganisation).
- **Batrec**, Niesenstrasse 44, 3752 Wimmis — [batrec.ch](https://batrec.ch/de/).
- Whether Swiss Post branches take batteries is **unverified**; INOBAT leaflets list them (search snippet only).
- **BattDG** (in force 7 October 2025, replacing the BattG) — [BattDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/battdg/BJNR0E90B0025.html):
  - §6: end users collect waste batteries separately.
  - §14: retailers take back portable waste batteries free of charge, limited to the types they stock.
  - §15: municipal waste authorities accept household portable batteries free of charge, whatever their condition.
  - §30, §37: the Umweltbundesamt (UBA) is the authority, and may delegate to stiftung ear.
- **ElektroG §10(1):** before handing in an old device, end users remove batteries that the device does not enclose — [ElektroG §10](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/elektrog_2015/__10.html).
- **UBA guidance** (updated 24 October 2023): hand in damaged batteries promptly and tell the staff; tape the terminals; use a sealable container with sand; never reuse or open them — [UBA, lithium batteries](https://www.umweltbundesamt.de/en/umwelttipps-fuer-den-alltag/proper-handling-of-lithium-batteries-rechargeable).
- **GRS safety standard** (September 2023): short-circuit protection is mandatory for damaged Li batteries, for example by taping the terminals, and staff need ADR training — [GRS Merkblatt](https://www.grs-pro.de/fileadmin/Downloads/Sicherheit/GRS-Merkblatt_Sicherheitsstandard_Sep2023_ws23200.pdf). Secondary: GRS's service list of 17 December 2025 puts damaged cells in the "Sicherheitsstandard rot" case (270 kg per pickup, €650 deposit) — [GRS Leistungsverzeichnis](https://www.grs-batterien.de/fileadmin/Downloads/Registrierung_Ruecknahmestelle/GRS_Leistungsverzeichnis_LV_17122025.pdf).
- Swiss practice for dismantlers, secondary: [SENS eRecycling recommendation on lithium batteries (2025)](https://erecycling-prod.mironet.ch/dam/jcr:19ebdf41-0517-4253-9e43-c08b459bcaf1/250324-SENS-eRecycling-Empfehlung-Umgang-mit-Lithiumbatterien-bei-der-Demontage_DE.2025-08-11-08-36-24.pdf).

## 4. Customs and VAT

### Takeaway
**Into Switzerland:**
- Li-ion cells are tariff number 8507.6000, statistical key 911 for items of 5 kg or less. Swiss duty is 0; industrial tariffs were abolished on 1 January 2024.
- Import VAT is 8.1 % on goods plus transport. It is not collected when the tax is CHF 5 or less, which is goods worth about CHF 62.
- Swiss Post adds CHF 13 + 3 % for clearance, capped at CHF 70; couriers charge more.
- Foreign sellers and platforms with at least CHF 100,000 a year of Swiss small-parcel sales charge Swiss VAT at checkout.

**Into Germany:**
- The badge's tariff heading is uncertain (8543.70, 8531.20 or 9405.61). Swiss duty is 0 under any of them.
- German import VAT is 19 %. EU duty is 0 % on 8543 70 00 and 8531 20 20, and 2.7 % on loose cells (8507 60).
- Swiss origin gives 0 % under the 1972 EU–CH agreement if the origin rules are met. Assembling the boards in Switzerland probably meets them, but "simple assembly" does not count.
- Hundreds of badges exceed the travellers' allowance and must be declared.
- An ATA carnet (temporary admission) cannot be used for goods sold or given away.

### Cited findings
- **Swiss tariff** (BAZG open data, updated 10 September 2026) — [tariff structure CSV](https://ocean.bazg.admin.ch/open-data-reports/TN_CONTROLCODE_STRUCTURE_v1/TN_CONTROLCODE_STRUCTURE_v1.csv):
  - 8507.6000 "Lithium-Ionen-Akkumulatoren": key 911 for items of 5 kg or less.
  - Candidates for the badge: 8543.7000 (electrical apparatus with their own function, not elsewhere specified); 8531.2000 (indicator panels with LCD or LED); 9405.6100 (illuminated signs, which explicitly include "illuminated name-plates").
  - No binding ruling for a wearable LED badge was found (**unverified**). To be certain, ask BAZG (zolltarif@bazg.admin.ch) or apply for an EU binding tariff information (BTI).
- **Industrial tariffs abolished** from 1 January 2024 (chapters 25–97). Origin proofs are still needed for re-export to free-trade partners — [BAZG](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/aufhebung-industriezolle-januar-2024).
- **VAT de minimis.** "Steuerbeträge bis 5 Schweizerfranken nicht erhoben": a tax base of CHF 62 at 8.1 % — [BAZG, Briefe und Pakete](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/empfangen-von-briefen-und-paketen).
- **Swiss Post clearance.** CHF 13.00 from the EU or CHF 16.00 from elsewhere, plus 3 % of the value, at most CHF 70.00 — [Post, Importverzollung](https://www.post.ch/de/empfangen/importverzollung).
- **Mail-order and platform rule.** Sellers with at least CHF 100,000 a year of small-consignment sales make domestic supplies (Art. 7 Abs. 3 MWSTG, Art. 4a MWSTV, since 2019). The same applies to platforms since 1 January 2025 — [ESTV](https://www.estv.admin.ch/de/mwst-versandhandel-und-plattformbesteuerung), [BAZG](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/de/regelung-mwst-versandhandel-und-plattformbesteuerung). Which platforms (AliExpress, Alibaba, LCSC) are registered is **unverified**.
- **EU TARIC for Swiss origin** (queried 17 October 2026) — [TARIC 8507600090, CH](https://ec.europa.eu/taxation_customs/dds2/taric/measures.jsp?Lang=en&Taric=8507600090&Area=CH&SimDate=20261017):
  - 8507 60 00 90: third-country duty 2.70 %, Swiss preference 0 %.
  - 8543 70 00 and 8531 20 20: 0 %.
  - 9405 61 00: the third-country rate was not shown (**unverified**).
  - A low-value consignment duty of EUR 3.00 is listed for 1 July–31 October 2026 (Council Regulation (EU) 2026/382, consignments of €150 or less). It matters if single badges or cells are posted into the EU.
- **German import VAT** is 19 % — [UStG §12](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ustg_1980/__12.html).
  - The travellers' allowance (€300 by land, €430 by air) covers only imports of a non-commercial character whose quantity does not suggest commercial purposes. Hundreds of badges do not qualify — [Directive 2007/74/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32007L0074).
- **Origin (revised PEM Convention, in force 1 January 2025)** — [Decision (EU) 2024/390](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec/2024/390/oj):
  - For chapter 85 (which covers 8507, 8531 and 8543) and for chapter 94, a product qualifies by a change of heading, or by non-originating materials of at most 50 % of the ex-works price.
  - "Simple assembly … to constitute a complete article" is not enough.
  - Any exporter may make the origin declaration on the invoice for consignments up to €6,000.
  - Assessment, not verified: PCB assembly and programming in Switzerland from Chinese parts of other headings probably meets the change-of-heading test. Chinese cells carried as loose spares stay non-originating and pay 2.7 %.
- **ATA carnet**, secondary: "Für Verkaufsware oder Waren zur Gratisabgabe … ist das Carnet ATA nicht verwendbar" (an ATA carnet cannot be used for goods for sale or giveaway) — [Zurich exhibition customs leaflet](https://www.hrfestival.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/zollformalitaeten_Exhibitions_ZH_D.pdf).
